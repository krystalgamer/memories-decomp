## `func_80028B08` at 0x80028B08

`gcc_2_8_1_g8_split`, **384 instructions against 384**, **172 differing
positions**. Written from scratch this session; there was no previous
candidate and no inventory note. Recorded early, per this file's purpose,
so the next attempt starts from the code rather than from the prose.

The passes below are kept in the order they happened, because two of them
reached figures that later turned out to be measured against a structurally
wrong candidate, and that is the most useful thing this entry records. The
headline above is the state the source below reproduces; intermediate figures
in the prose are historical and are labelled where they no longer hold.

The duel field card-detail panel. Returns immediately unless the object's
`field_4` is non-negative and bit 0x40 of the window record's `field_8` is
set. Bit 0x4 additionally republishes the object from the window record and
bails on a non-positive `func_80041F90`. It then fills a 24-byte parameter

detail section for a single bare call when it is at or above 0x14. Those are
two different fields, which the first two passes got wrong.

**Two levers so far, both structural, and together worth 71 positions.**

- *The three scratchpad addresses are local pointer variables, not constants.*
  This is the one that matters. Retail saves **nine** callee-saved registers and
  holds `0x1F800320`, `0x1F800344` and `0x1F800398` in `$s1`, `$s6` and `$s7`
  across the whole body, with `$s8` taking the second parameter. Written as
  macros or casts in the expressions, GCC re-materialises `lui`/`ori` at each
  use and at each call site, uses one callee-saved register fewer, and shifts
  the entire prologue. Naming them as locals was worth 51 positions, 415 to
  364, and took the instruction count from +35 to +6. The mixed re-materialisation
  in the target - some call sites copy `$s1`, others rebuild the constant - is
  CSE acting on those locals, not evidence for constants.
- *The three assignments belong below the two early-exit guards.* Retail
  materialises them after both `return` tests; in the entry block they cost 20
  positions. 364 to 344, +6 to +5.

The second lever is a direct confirmation of the rule recorded in
`matching-evidence.md` this session: intra-block statement position is inert,
and the placements that pay are the ones that **cross a basic-block boundary**.
The move here crosses two guard edges, and it paid. That rule predicted both
that this would be worth trying and that shuffling the three assignments among
themselves would not be.

**Known remaining structure.** The count is still 344 with five extra
instructions, so this is not yet an allocation problem and should not be
attacked as one. The next things to read are the field widths in the parameter
block - `w`/`h` at +8 are written both as two `sh` and as one `sw`, and
`field_E`/`field_F` at +0xE have the same byte/word aliasing, currently spelled
with `*(u32 *)` casts - and the two digit loops, which are `do`/`while`
countdowns from 3 whose body ordering has not been checked against the target
at all.


**Second pass: seven more facts, read by alignment rather than by position.**

With five extra instructions a positional diff is the wrong instrument - every
position after the first insertion is wrong for a reason that is not local to
it. `tools/project/align_functions.py` (written for this) strips immediates and
runs `difflib.SequenceMatcher` over opcode-plus-register keys, so it reports
which instructions are *inserted or missing* rather than which offsets
disagree. That is what produced all of the following; none were visible
positionally.

- *The call site independently reproduces `func_80041F90`'s known arity.*
  The target materialises `0x1F800398` into `$a3` immediately before the call;
  `$a3` is caller-saved and the value is never read again, so on a
  three-argument signature those two instructions would be dead, and they are
  not. The fourth argument is the scratchpad pointer this unit already holds.
  This is a *check*, not a discovery: `display_object_projection.c` already
  defines the function with four parameters. Worth recording because arriving
  at a matched function's ABI from liveness alone confirms the definition from
  a direction that does not depend on it - but it says nothing new about the
  callee, and reading it as a recovery would overstate it.
- *`win`'s `field_30` and `field_32` are signed.* They appear as `lh` where they
  feed the call and `lhu` where they feed a halfword store; declared `u16` they
  are `lhu` in both places. The signed declaration reproduces both.
- *The object's `field_4` is held in a local across its two updates.* The target
  masks, stores, then or-s and stores again from the same register. Re-reading
  the member for the second update costs an extra load.
- *Four of the `w`/`h` pairs are single word stores.* `0x000D0006`, `0x00090009`,
  `0x00100010` and the copy of `obj->field_3C` are one `sw` each; the two at
  `0x66`/`0x60` and `0x60`/`0xE` are genuinely two `sh`. Both spellings occur in
  one function, so this is per site and not a struct-layout decision.
- *`field_E`/`field_F` is likewise one halfword store* where the source looks
  like two byte assignments. Written as two, GCC emits the shift and a second
  `sb`.
- *The guardian-star tile needs a local.* `rec->field_3B << 4` is computed once
  and used for both the byte store and the `field_10` sum. Written as the
  expression twice, the intervening stores may alias `rec`, so it is reloaded.
- *The clamped value and the loop counter are one variable.* The target holds
  both in `$s0`, and their live ranges are disjoint. As separate locals the
  clamp collapses into `$a0` and four instructions disappear, including the
  two `move $a0,$s0` copies that the delay slot duplicates.

**Open, and the most likely next lever: `$s6` and `$s7` are swapped.** The
target puts the `0x1F800344` pointer in `$s6` and `0x1F800398` in `$s7`; this
candidate has them the other way round, which costs a position at every one of
the nine call sites and at each field access. Neither declaration order nor
assignment order moves it - all three orders were measured and give the same
allocation - so this is an allocno-priority question and should be attacked
with reference counts rather than with statement position.

*Superseded:* this was called the highest-value remaining item, on a base that
was structurally wrong. Re-measured on the corrected candidate the inversion is
27 of 172 positions, so it is not dominant and was never the main obstacle.

The instruction count is now -4 rather than +35, so what remains is much closer
to an allocation and ordering problem than the first pass was, but it is not
there yet and should not be treated as one.


**Third pass: audit the opcode multiset before minimising anything.**

The state the two passes above reached was a local optimum arrived at through
three semantic errors, so every lever they measured was measured against the
wrong structure and none of their figures should be trusted as comparisons.
Scanning the aligned output for blocks whose *opcode* multisets differ - rather
than blocks that merely disagree, where a register difference is only an
allocation symptom - found all three, and fixing them took the function to
**178 with an exact instruction count**, and then to 172.

- *The parameter block's `+0x10` field is copied as a word.* The target does
  `lw 64(s3)` / `sw 16(s1)`; the candidate emitted a halfword pair, because
  `field_10` is `u16` and GCC narrowed the load. Written
  `*(u32 *)&PRM->field_10 = obj->field_40` the site matches exactly - and the
  total *rises* to 323, which is why it had survived four passes of tuning.
- *The card record is indexed by `field_67`, not `field_68`.* The target loads
  `0x67` and shifts it by 6 for the `D_800EA0E8` subscript, and loads `0x68`
  separately for the `< 0x14` guard. The candidate used one field for both,
  which is why it was an `lbu` short.
- *The `0xFFFF` in the row advance must be a value, not a folded immediate.*
  The target materialises it and uses `addu`; written as a literal, GCC folds
  `+ 0xFFFF` into `addiu -1`, which is arithmetically identical and
  structurally wrong. Naming it, or writing the addend `(u32)PRM->h + 0xFFFFU`,
  both produce the register form. **This one is worth 146 positions**, 324 to
  178, and it is what brings the instruction count to exact.

That last figure is the argument for auditing opcodes first. A one-instruction
opcode discrepancy was holding 146 positions of ordering in place, and no
amount of reordering or reallocation could have found it, because the count
kept saying the wrong structure was better.

**What is left is 178 positions with an exact instruction count and an opcode
multiset out by two:** the target has two `nop`s where the candidate has two
`li`s, both of them the `0xF8` colour constant being rematerialised where the
target still has it live in `$s4`. That is the next thing to attack, and it is
now a genuine allocation question rather than a structural one.


**Fourth pass: store order against an aliasing load is not covered by the
intra-block rule.**

`notes/research/matching-evidence.md` records, from `func_80023144`, that
statement position within a basic block is exactly inert. That is true for
*independent* instructions, which is what was measured there, and it is not
true here.

The object's `field_44` assignment sits between two stores to its `field_4`.
Moving it up one statement, so that it falls between the mask and the store
rather than after both, is worth 6 positions: 178 to **172**. Nothing else
changed. The reason it works is that these are stores to memory the compiler
cannot prove disjoint from the loads around them, so the scheduler is not free
to reorder them - source order *is* the dependence order. Placing the same
assignment before the whole group is equally good; reading `field_4` before the
`field_20` store costs 175.

So the rule needs its scope stated rather than restated: **intra-block source
order is inert for instructions the scheduler may reorder, and load-bearing
for stores and loads it may not.** Both halves are now measured on real
functions, and the second is the reason a candidate can look "already
minimised" and still have easy positions in it.

**Re-measuring the levers on the corrected base, which the third pass said was
necessary.** The `0xF8` colour constant is a good example of why. On the wrong
base, naming it at all five sites scored 314 and at only the three sites the
target's `$s4` covers scored 378. On the corrected base the same two choices
score **172** and **373**. The ordering survived, but the gap did not mean what
it appeared to, and the second figure is now the clearer statement: copying the
target's register usage site by site is not merely unhelpful, it is 200
positions worse than either naming the constant everywhere or nowhere.

The `$s6`/`$s7` inversion is 27 of 172 on this base, having been 40 of 314 on
the old one - still not the dominant residual, and still unexplained.

*The corrected ordering rule also works in the other direction, which is worth
one line because it saved a build.* The `arg` computation ends in an `or` whose
operands the target holds the other way round from this candidate: retail has
the masked value in `$v1` and the `0xF0000` in `$v0`, the candidate the
reverse. Swapping the operands in the source - at one site or at both - leaves
the differing set **byte-identical**, positions 59 and 62 to 65 included. Those
two operands are independent computation with no memory operation between them,
which is precisely the case the rule says is inert, so the rule predicted the
result rather than merely explaining it afterwards.
