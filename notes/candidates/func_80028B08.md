**Two levers so far, both structural, and together worth 71 positions.**

- *The three scratchpad addresses are local pointer variables, not constants.*
  This is the one that matters. Retail saves **nine** callee-saved registers and
  holds `0x1F800320`, `0x1F800344` and `0x1F800398` in `$s1`, `$s6` and `$s7`
  across the whole body, with `$s8` taking the second parameter. Written as
  macros or casts in the expressions, GCC re-materialises `lui`/`ori` at each
  use and at each call site, uses one callee-saved register fewer, and shifts
  the entire prologue. Naming them as locals was worth 51 positions, 415 to
  364, and took the instruction count from +35 to +6. The mixed re-materialisation
  in the target - some call sites copy `$s1`, others rebuild the constant - is
  CSE acting on those locals, not evidence for constants.
- *The three assignments belong below the two early-exit guards.* Retail
  materialises them after both `return` tests; in the entry block they cost 20
  positions. 364 to 344, +6 to +5.

The second lever is a direct confirmation of the rule recorded in
`matching-evidence.md` this session: intra-block statement position is inert,
and the placements that pay are the ones that **cross a basic-block boundary**.
The move here crosses two guard edges, and it paid. That rule predicted both
that this would be worth trying and that shuffling the three assignments among
themselves would not be.

**Second pass: seven more facts, read by alignment rather than by position.**

With five extra instructions a positional diff is the wrong instrument - every
position after the first insertion is wrong for a reason that is not local to
it. `tmp/analysis/align.py` (written for this) strips immediates and runs

instructions are *inserted or missing* rather than which offsets disagree.
That is what produced all of the following; none were visible positionally.

  `0x1F800398` into `$a3` immediately before the call. `$a3` is caller-saved and
  the value is never read again, so as a three-argument call those two
  instructions are dead - which they are not. The fourth argument is the
  scratchpad pointer this unit already holds.

  feed the call and `lhu` where they feed a halfword store; declared `u16` they
  are `lhu` in both places. The signed declaration reproduces both.

  masks, stores, then or-s and stores again from the same register. Re-reading
  the member for the second update costs an extra load.
- *Four of the `w`/`h` pairs are single word stores.* `0x000D0006`, `0x00090009`,
  `0x00100010` and the copy of `obj->field_3C` are one `sw` each; the two at
  `0x66`/`0x60` and `0x60`/`0xE` are genuinely two `sh`. Both spellings occur in
  one function, so this is per site and not a struct-layout decision.
- *`field_E`/`field_F` is likewise one halfword store* where the source looks
  like two byte assignments. Written as two, GCC emits the shift and a second
  `sb`.
- *The guardian-star tile needs a local.* `rec->field_3B << 4` is computed once
  and used for both the byte store and the `field_10` sum. Written as the

- *The clamped value and the loop counter are one variable.* The target holds
  both in `$s0`, and their live ranges are disjoint. As separate locals the
  clamp collapses into `$a0` and four instructions disappear, including the
  two `move $a0,$s0` copies that the delay slot duplicates.

**Open, and the most likely next lever: `$s6` and `$s7` are swapped.** The
target puts the `0x1F800344` pointer in `$s6` and `0x1F800398` in `$s7`; this

assignment order moves it - all three orders were measured and give the same
allocation - so this is an allocno-priority question and should be attacked
with reference counts rather than with statement position. That is the single
highest-value remaining item.
