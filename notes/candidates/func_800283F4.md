`reorg.c` fills call delay slots before jump delay slots, scanning backwards
from the call; whichever of the two `li`s sits closest to the `jal` wins the
call slot and the other is left for the branch. Retail's RTL therefore had
`li a0,3` *before* `li s0,-1024`, which no source ordering reaches: the
argument setup is emitted at the call, so a source-level assignment to the
slide value always lands ahead of it. Tried and rejected: the assignment
before the flag store, after it, immediately before the call, after the call,
before the enclosing `if` (which moves the `li` into the prologue), binding the
channel index 3 to a local and to an `$a0`-pinned local so it would be
materialised early (gcc constant-propagates both away), `s16` instead of `s32`
for the pinned value, and pinning to `$s1`. A sweep of all 31 profiles keeps
`gcc_2_8_1_g8_split` at 2 and puts every other profile at 6 or worse.

### Re-measured, and three corrections to the reading above

Reproduced exactly: `gcc_2_8_1_g8_split`, 334/334, 2 differing words.

**The branch delay slot's `addiu $a0, $zero, 0x3` *is* the first call's
argument.** Retail materialises the constant twice -- once at `+0x18` and again
at `+0x30` for `func_80029164` -- so the slot is not holding an unrelated
value. That means the branch slot is an *eager* fill of the fall-through
thread's first instruction, and the requirement is precisely that `li $a0, 3`
be the first instruction of the block after the branch.

**The `$s0` pin is orthogonal to the delay slots.** Unpinned, the build has the
slide value in `$s1` and everything shifts to 58 differing words as recorded --
but the two delay slots are inverted in *exactly the same way*, `li $s1, -1024`
in the branch slot and `li $a0, 3` in the call slot. So the pin buys the
register naming and nothing else here; it is not what strands the argument, and
removing it does not open the slot up.

**All six orderings of the three statements are now measured**, not four:

| order | result |
| --- | --- |
| `slide`, flag, call | 2 |
| flag, `slide`, call | 2 |
| flag, call, `slide` | 5 |
| `slide`, call, flag | 7 |
| call, `slide`, flag | 331, one long |
| call, flag, `slide` | 331, one long |

Moving the assignment after the call does put `li $s0` beyond the call, but it
takes the constant out of the call slot rather than into it, and the branch
slot then takes `li $v0, 1`.

**What the residual actually requires.** `reorg` fills the call slot by
scanning backwards for the last movable instruction, and fills the branch slot
eagerly from the first instruction of the fall-through thread. Retail's block
is `li $a0, 3` / `li $v0, 1` / `lui $at` / `sb` / `li $s0, -1024`, so both slots
fall out of one fact: the argument setup sits at the *front* of the block,
above the flag store. Argument setup is emitted immediately before its call, so
reaching that needs the post-reload scheduler to hoist it over the store, and
no statement order makes it do so -- in every ordering above the build leaves
the two `li`s in emission order. This is a scheduler decision with no source
handle, not a statement-order problem, and it should not be re-attacked from
the ordering side.

### Levers that got it here

- **`slide` has to be pinned to `$s0`.** Unpinned, gcc gives the slide value
  `$s1` and the display object `$s0`, the opposite of retail, and the whole
  function shifts: 58 differing words instead of 2.
- **The two pad tests must not cross-jump.** Written as two plain
  `if ((... & 0x20) == 0) return;` arms, gcc merges the shared
  `andi`/`beqz`/`nop` and the build comes out five instructions short. Writing
  the first arm as `if ((...) != 0) goto press; return;` keeps both copies,
  which is what retail has.
- **Keep a flag value live across the store that follows it.** Two blocks read
  `D_8009B248`, modify it and store it while also storing through the object
  pointer. Spelled `D_8009B248 |= 0x20;` after the object store, the ORI lands
  in `$v0` and cross-jumping merges the `sb` with the `|= 0x50` store at the
  end of the function; computing `state = D_8009B248 | 0x20;` *before* the
  object store keeps it in `$v1` across that store and the merge does not
  happen.
- **Short-lived reads of a global pointer need their own locals.** Four of
  them here: the caption box for `TextBox_SetPos`, the box for
  `TextBox_Destroy`, the background object across the exit tests and the
  destroy call, and the `D_8009B248` read in the closing block. Sharing one
  local across two of these costs an extra `move` into `$a0` each time, and
  sharing the closing-block read with the rest of the function moved the whole
  `andi`/`andi`/`sb` triple.
- **The fade-in latch tests `< 0x40`, not `>= 0x40`.** `sltiu` followed by
  `beqz` to the epilogue means the body runs when the byte is below the bound;
  the counter starts at 0x80 and wraps.
- **Read a global into a local before a store that would block it.** Both
  `D_8009B24B` before the first object's field stores and `D_8009B24C` before
  the `D_8009B240 + 0x60` store in the close path; the second is what lets the
  next call's argument load fill the load-delay slot instead of a `nop`.
- The argument to `func_80043230` is the object's `+0x60` counter, already in
  `$a3` from the test that guards the call; the four-argument prototype in
  `display_object_interpolation.c` is the right one.
