**Reuse and bindings settle the lookup and shared tail.** Reusing `t2` for

**A halfword binding restores the formerly missing copy.** Bind `stop_arg`
to `$a0` as `u16`, and initialize it from the original `arg0` parameter before
the branch, not from the `$t0`-bound `id`. This was motivated by GCC 2.8.1's
different treatment of pseudo-register and hard-register self-copies in

## The two exchanged copies are a first-pass scheduling decision

Re-measured with relocations resolved: still exactly two differing positions,
and they are the two entry copies the entry above describes.

What is new is that the source *can* produce the target's order, and that a
later pass takes it away again. Compiled with `-fno-schedule-insns` and
`-fno-delayed-branch`, the unscheduled stream follows source order exactly:

```
stop_arg then idc      move $4,$8 ; move $9,$8      (the stored source)
idc then stop_arg      move $9,$8 ; move $4,$8      (the target's order)
```

So writing `idc = id;` before `stop_arg = arg0;` builds the target's
arrangement, and the first scheduling pass then reorders both spellings back to
`$4` first. The delay-slot filler is downstream of that: it takes whichever
copy ends up last before the branch, which is why the two positions always
exchange together rather than one moving on its own.

**This is not a tie-break that source order can reach.** `rank_for_schedule`
falls back to original insn order only when priority and dependence class both
tie, and here they do not: swapping the two statements changes the unscheduled
order and leaves the scheduled order identical. Something about `$4` -- an
argument hard register with a use in the fall-through block -- outranks `$9`.

**`-fno-schedule-insns` is not the answer even though it produces the order.**
The same source under `gcc_2_8_1_g0_no_sched1` builds 66 instructions against
68 and 51 differing positions, because the pass also settles the register
allocation for the whole body: `$v0`/`$v1` exchange through the table lookup,
`g_SDValue` lands in `$t0` instead of `$a1`, and two instructions disappear.
Read it as the diagnostic that identifies the pass, not as a profile to ship --
the same conclusion the `func_80045208` entry reaches about `-fno-schedule-insns2`.

Crossed without improving on two: both orders of the two assignments, both with
`arg0` and with `id` as the source; the condition hoisted into a temporary
before either copy; the `stop_arg` assignment moved inside the branch, which
lets combine fold the copy and the mask into one `andi` and costs an
instruction; `stop_arg` and `idc` unpinned individually and together; `idc`
moved to `$10` and `stop_arg` to `$5`; and the full lever sweep at depth two
over the three `-G0` profiles.

## Exact scheduling diagnostic and policy boundary

A zero-byte compiler dependency proves the rest of the candidate is exact.
The reproducible source is
`tmp/decompile-20260907/80048658-a0-alias-s32-after-id-init.c`, SHA-256
`db2e7a7472cc44b6a9cd016b0823f7e8c37f90a2eb127f4e5d246e560ddd99d6`.
It builds all 272 bytes and all eight relocations exactly with
`gcc_2_8_1_g0`.

The entry uses two sequential views of `$a0`. A full-width view captures the
incoming argument, then an empty read/write dependency keeps the `$t1` and
`$a2` copies before the halfword view is rebuilt:

```c
register u16 stop_arg asm("$4");
register s32 incoming_a0 asm("$4");

id = arg0;
incoming_a0 = arg0;
pan = arg2;
idc = id;
vol = arg1;
asm("" : "+r"(idc), "+r"(vol), "+r"(incoming_a0));
stop_arg = arg0;
```

Before delayed-branch filling, this gives
`t0, a3, t1, a2, <zero-byte dependency>, a0, test, branch`; reorg then places
the `$a0 = $t0` copy in the branch delay slot. Removing the dependency returns
to the score-10 order. Using an uninitialized `$a0` input also matches, but was
discarded; the source above initializes every C variable before use.

This is diagnostic rather than promotable evidence. Post-terminal recording
and integration deliberately permit register-variable pins but reject
statement-level inline assembly. The exact barrier therefore cannot be used to
close the deferred history without changing repository policy.
