### Re-measured

The stored source still reproduces exactly: 68/68 instructions with the same
two words exchanged, and every other word identical once relocations resolve.
Both differing words are `addu $rX, $t0, $zero`, so the whole residue is which
of the two copies of `$t0` the delay-slot filler takes for
`beqz $v0, .L80048690`. Retail leaves the `$t1` copy before the branch and puts
the `$a0` copy in the slot.

Further negatives measured on this source, none of which move it:

- **Every `gcc_2_8_1_*` profile** gives the same two words.
- **Making the copy belong to the taken block.** Moving `stop_arg = arg0`
  inside the `if (id & 0x8000)` body is the shape that would let `dbr` lift it
  into the slot from the fall-through side, but the copy is then redundant --
  `$a0` still holds `arg0` at that point -- and GCC elides it, giving 67
  instructions. This is the same effect the note records for the 66-instruction
  source, and it is why the copy has to be made before the branch.
- **Widths.** `idc` as `u16` or `s16`, `stop_arg` as `s16`, and `id` as `u32`
  all hold at two. `stop_arg` as `u32` loses the copy again, which confirms the
  HI-mode binding is what retains it.
- **Copy sources.** `idc = arg0` instead of `idc = id` holds at two;
  `stop_arg = id` instead of `stop_arg = arg0` costs two more.
- **Initialisation order.** All orderings of the five entry assignments hold at
  two or worse, and exchanging the two declarations changes nothing.

So the residue is unchanged: both copies have no consumer inside their own
block, so they carry equal scheduling priority and are ordered by their
incoming RTL order, and no source-level control found so far reaches that
order.
