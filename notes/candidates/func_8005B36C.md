## Seventeen down to five: separate the load from the increment, and name the loaded word

**`*s++` fuses the load and the bump, and the target does not fuse them.** The
target loads with `lw $a0,0($t0)` third from the top and bumps `$t0` much later,

*s++` GCC emits the `addiu` early and sinks the load, which is the reverse.
Spelling it `D_800FE240[0] = s[0];` with `from = s + 1;` at the copy loop puts
the load where the target has it. Worth six positions, seventeen to eleven.

the first word of the primitive in `$a0` and the `D_800FE240` pointer in `$v1`;

target's assignment. Eleven to five. Note this shares `$4` with the existing

## Five down to two: the pins were the obstacle, not the lever

The five-position state above kept `s` pinned to `$8` and `first` pinned to
`$4`, because those pins were what recovered the target's register assignment
from the original seventeen. Once the *shape* is right they are no longer
carrying anything, and each one costs.

- Dropping the `s` pin, with `s++` written as its own statement, fixes the two
  increment positions **and** the word load's base register at the same time,
  because GCC then keeps the pointer in `$t0` for both loads instead of reading
  through the still-live parameter register. Five to four.
- Dropping the `first` pin takes the `lbu`/`lw` pair into the target's order.
  Four to two.
- The `from` pin turns out to be inert either way and is dropped for clarity.

**The general point is worth keeping.** A pin that was necessary to reach an
intermediate state is not necessarily part of the answer: it can be
compensating for a shape problem, and once that problem is fixed the pin
becomes the thing holding the last positions in place. Re-test every pin after
any structural change rather than carrying them forward.

**What is left is two positions**, on an exact instruction multiset with
everything else in place: the two parameter saves at the top are exchanged.
The target emits `move $t0,$a0` then `move $t3,$a1`; the build emits them the
other way round. Both are independent copies of argument registers into
callee-saved ones, and `$a1` has to be freed early in both builds because the
mask arithmetic uses it as a temporary. Nothing reaches it: four placements of
the `table` assignment, removing the `table` local so the parameter is used
directly, three declaration orders, pins on `table`, `dst` and `from`, and
`-fno-expensive-optimizations`, both scheduling flags, `-fno-delayed-branch`,
`-fno-cse-follow-jumps`, `-fno-thread-jumps`, `-fno-strength-reduce`,
`-fno-peephole` and `-fno-caller-saves` all leave `move $11,$5` first.
