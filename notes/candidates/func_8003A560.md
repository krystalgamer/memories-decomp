## `func_8003A560` at 0x8003A560

`gcc_2_8_1_cc_g8_as_g1_split`, 240 of 240 instructions, **opcode multiset
distance 0**, 12 differing words. Written from scratch: no stored candidate, no
rows in `external_attempts.csv`, empty inventory note.

Swaps an effect's VRAM page in and out around a memory-card style transition.
`D_8015C410` is a five-entry table of resident page ids, `D_8009B326` records
the last free slot in it, and `D_80010000` points at the backing store whose
pages are `0x18C10` bytes apart. Each page carries its own scratch area at
`+0x18C00`, where the two `RECT`s handed to `LoadImage`/`StoreImage` are built,
and an `0x800`-byte block at `+0x18400` that is copied to or from
`D_801AF000`/`D_801AF800` depending on `p[0x3C]`.

On the first tick (`func_80039F1C(p) == 0`) it walks the table from index 4
down. If the effect's page id (`+0x30`) is already resident it uploads that
page and sets bit 6 of `+0x33`; otherwise, provided no transfer is in flight,
it queues one through `File_TryRequestAsyncTransfer` with `func_8003A01C` as
the callback and the sector `p[0x30] * 50 + 0x3B4E`. On a later tick with bit 6
clear it claims the recorded free slot and does the reverse with `StoreImage`;
once bit 6 is set it hands off to `func_8003A1EC` and moves the flag to `+0x32`.

## Six things the shape needed

**The profile is `cc_g8_as_g1_split`, and the -G split is what identifies it.**
Retail reads `D_8009B0F4` with `lui $v1, %hi(...)` / `lw $v1, %lo(...)` into
the *same* register and writes it with `lui $at` / `sw %lo($at)` - both the
assembler's macro expansion, i.e. gcc did not split that address. It *did*
split `D_8015C410` (`lui $v1, %hi` then `addiu $a1, $v1, %lo`, two different
registers). Under -G8 a sized `extern s32` is small data, so gcc leaves its
address to the assembler; an unsized `extern s8 x[]` is not, so gcc splits it.
That fixes the compiler at -G8. The assembler must then be at -G1 or -G2:
`D_8009B326` is one byte and comes out `%gp_rel($gp)`, while `D_8009B0F4` is
four and comes out through `$at`. -G0 loses the `%gp_rel`, -G4 and -G8 turn the
four-byte globals gp-relative as well.

**`D_80010000` must be read into a local before the loop.** The loop stores to
`D_8009B326`, so the load is not loop-invariant to gcc and it re-issues the
`lui`/`lw` on every iteration, recomputing the page pointer from scratch inside
the `bgez` delay slot. Reading it once into `base` makes `base + i * 0x18C10`
a giv of `i` whose `add_val` is a register, which is retail's
`lui $v0, 0x6` / `ori $v0, 0x3040` / `addu $s2, $v1, $v0` initialiser and its
`addu $s2, $s2, $v0` decrement.

**The table base has to go through a local too, and the subscript has to be
written index-first.** `D_8015C410[i]` strength-reduces to a walking pointer;
`tbl[i]` with `s8 *tbl = D_8015C410;` leaves the address as a mult-1 giv that
gcc does not reduce, which is retail's `addu $v1, $a0, $a1` recomputed each
iteration. Spelling it `*(s8 *)(i + (s32)tbl)` rather than `tbl[i]` is what
puts the index in the first `addu` operand; `tbl[i]`, `*(i + tbl)` and
`*(s8 *)(i + (s32)D_8015C410)` all give `addu $v1, $a1, $a0`.

**The two halves of the function need their own page and scratch locals.**
Sharing one `slot`/`img` pair between the load and the store path makes the
giv's destination live outside the loop, so `strength_reduce` cannot replace it
outright and leaves `move $s2, $a1` in the `bgez` delay slot with the giv
itself in a caller-saved register. Two pairs (`slot`/`img` and `vram`/`buf`)
drop the copy and put the giv in `$s2` where retail has it.

**Each rectangle's variable field has to be computed into a local before that
rectangle's constant fields are stored.** Retail issues `lbu $a2, 0x3C($s3)`
*before* the `sh`s that precede it in program order, which gcc can only do if
the load is ahead of the stores in the RTL - it cannot move a load across a
store to an unrelated pointer. Written inline in the `+0xC00` store the `lbu`
lands after the two `0x100` stores and the schedule drifts for the rest of the
block. `w = 0x340 - p[0x3C] * 0xC0;` before the four stores fixes the first
rectangle in the load path, and `y = p[0x3C] * 2 + 0xF0;` between the `+0xC08`
store and the `+0xC0C`/`+0xC0E` pair fixes the second in both paths. The store
path's first rectangle wants the *other* form, `c = p[0x3C];` before the stores
with the arithmetic left inline, because there the `lbu` sits between the
argument copies and the scratch pointer rather than ahead of them. Getting
these four placements right took the diff from 52 words to 13.

**The block copy is written twice, once per branch.** In the load path only the
source pointer differs, and retail's single `addu $a1, $s2, $s1` after the
`j`/`addiu` pair is what cross-jumping leaves of two identical calls. In the
store path retail keeps `ori $a0, 0x8400` / `addu $a0, $s2, $a0` in *both*
branches, so the destination is spelled out in each.

## What is left is two registers and a preheader permutation

Six of the twelve differing words are one register swap. Retail allocates
`$s0` to the scratch pointer, `$s1` to the hoisted `0x18400`, `$s2` to the page
giv and `$s4` to the hoisted `0x100`; this build agrees on `$s0` and `$s4` and
swaps `$s1` and `$s2`. The other six are the preheader: both schedules issue
the same eleven instructions and both split one two-instruction constant around
the `D_80010000` load to cover its delay, but retail splits the `0x18400`
movable and issues the giv's `0x63040` first, while this build does the
reverse. That ordering follows from `move_movables` inserting before
`emit_iv_add_mult` at the loop start, so the movable is always the earlier of
the two in the RTL here, and the list scheduler takes it first.

Tried and rejected for the register swap: pinning the page pointer to `$s2`
(brings back the `strength_reduce` copy), pinning the scratch pointer to `$s0`,
six permutations of the three preheader statements, three declaration orders,
writing the page expression inline at all four uses instead of through a local,
walking the page pointer as an explicit biv with `slot -= 0x18C10` in the
increment clause, and an explicit `s32 k = 0x18400;` local for the block
offset. All of them land on the same 12 or worse; nothing tried changes which
of the two pseudos the allocator reaches first.

### Opening pass: the residual is loop-setup, not the named locals

Twelve of the sixteen positions are a clean `$s1`/`$s2` exchange: retail keeps
the strength-reduced slot pointer in `$s2` and the `0x18400` constant in `$s1`,
the candidate has them the other way round, and every later `addu` inherits it.
The remaining four are the order in which the loop preheader materialises its
constants - retail computes `0x63040` first, then the table address, then
`0x18400`, then loads `D_80010000`; the candidate does the table address first
and `0x63040` fourth.

**These are compiler-generated induction values, not named locals.** Pinning
`slot` to `$s2` is neutral and pinning `base` to `$s1` costs 20, which is the
evidence that the exchange is not reachable through the variables the source
declares. Making the walk explicit - hoisting `slot` out of the loop and
decrementing it by `0x18C10` - costs 3; parenthesising or integer-casting the
subscript is neutral; rewriting the loop as `i = 5; while (--i >= 0)` is
neutral.

**The `-G` setting is unusually load-bearing here.** Only `--expand-div -G1` and
`-G2` at the assembler reproduce 16; `-G0`, `-G4` and `-G8` all cost more than
200 positions, as does `gcc_2_8_1_g0` at 169 and the no-strength-reduce and
no-sched1 profiles at 213. That narrowness suggests the small-data threshold is
carrying a specific global into or out of `$gp` addressing, and it should be
checked before any further source work.
