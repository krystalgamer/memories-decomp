- *The byte scalars that are only referenced need pushing out of small data.*
  `D_8009B141` through `D_8009B144`, `D_8009B318` and `D_8009AFA2` are one byte

- *Index the table by its array name, not through a pointer variable.*
  `D_8009B0A0[i]` emits `addu $v0,$s1,$s3`, index before base, which is the
  target's operand order; a `u8 *` local holding the same address emits

- Three positions where the target materialises `&D_8009B0A0` *before* storing

worse. The difference from `func_8002FD10` is instructive: there the
mis-ranked pseudo was a `%hi` temporary with a one-instruction live range,
sitting at the top of the ranking where a single reference moves it past
several others. Here the two pseudos in question are a loop-invariant
gp-relative address hoisted by LICM and the loop's own induction variable,
both with long live ranges and low priorities, where the `floor_log2` cliff is
nowhere near. **The lever acts on short-lived, high-priority pseudos; it has
no purchase on long-lived ones.**

**The loop-rotation cluster is the `func_800283F4` mechanism, not a rotation
choice.** Reading the whole loop rather than the differing lines makes the
cause plain. The back branch is at `+0x1EC` and its target is the *second*
instruction of the body, so whichever instruction the body begins with is the
one `reorg` duplicates into the back-edge delay slot and rotates past. Retail's
body begins with `move $a0,$zero` / `move $a1,$a0`; the build's begins with
`addu $v0,$s1,$s3`. All five positions follow from that single fact.

So the requirement is that the call's two zero arguments be the first thing in
the loop body. That is the same requirement `func_800283F4` (0x800283F4) fails
on, and it fails here for the same reason: **GCC sinks argument setup to the
call and constant-propagates anything that would have materialised it early.**

The entry above records that source position for the address was tried in

That is the useful negative. The conflict between early materialisation and

motion creates it rather than by anything the source says. The register is
already `$s3` in both, so unlike `func_8002FD10` there is no allocation

**Both clusters are therefore closed, and the function is bounded at eight.**
It needs two instructions moved to the front of their blocks, one an address

### The distance is one instruction, and it is a loop rotation

The closing loop branches with `bgez $s1, +0x1C0`, so `+0x1C0` is the loop top
and `+0x1F0` is the branch's delay slot. The two builds rotate the loop
differently and therefore put different instructions in that slot:

```
retail                          build
+0x1BC  move  $a0, $zero        addu  $v0, $s1, $s3
+0x1C0  move  $a1, $a0    <-top addiu $s1, $s1, -1   <-top
+0x1C4  addu  $v0, $s1, $s3     move  $a0, $zero
+0x1C8  lw    $a2, 428($gp)     lw    $a2, 428($gp)
+0x1CC  addiu $s1, $s1, -1      move  $a1, $a0
  ...
+0x1EC  bgez  $s1, +0x1C0       bgez  $s1, +0x1C0
+0x1F0  move  $a0, $zero  <-slot addu $v0, $s1, $s3  <-slot
```

`move $a0, $zero` is `addu $a0, $zero, $zero`, so an instruction that the
disassembler prints as `move` is an `addu` in the encoding. That is why the
whole multiset difference is exactly one `addu` traded for one `move`: retail's
delay slot re-zeroes `$a0` every iteration, and the build's recomputes
`$v0 = $s1 + $s3` instead. Both are correct loop bodies; they are rotations of
each other.

So the two clusters are one cause. The five positions from `+0x1BC` to `+0x1F0`
are the rotation, and the remaining three at `+0x138`-`+0x140` are a separate
three-instruction rotation around the `gp`-relative stores. Neither is an
allocation disagreement - every register in the loop already matches.

### Cluster A: the address has two reachable slots and retail's is neither

The three positions at `+0x138`-`+0x140` are the order of three instructions in
the block that follows `jal func_80085320`:

```
retail   addiu $s3,$gp,408   sb $v0,420($gp)   andi $v1,$v0,0xff
build    sb $v0,420($gp)     andi $v1,$v0,0xff  addiu $s3,$gp,408
```

`addiu $s3,$gp,408` materialises `&D_8009B0A0`, which is used only in the
closing loop. The `sb` and `andi` both consume the call's return value; the
`addiu` consumes nothing and has no consumer inside the block, so GCC issues it
last. Retail issues it first.

Naming the address as an explicit `register u8 *src asm("$19")` and moving its
assignment is the obvious lever, and it has exactly **two** outcomes:

| where `src = D_8009B0A0` is written | where `addiu $s3` lands |
| --- | --- |
| after `func_80085320()` returns - five placements tried | last in the post-call block |
| before the call - two placements tried | *before* the `jal`, one slot too early |

The second is the interesting one. It does not merely score the same: it moves
the cluster to `+0x130`-`+0x138` and **fixes the `sb` and `andi`**, leaving only
the `addiu` misplaced. So the block order is reachable and the assignment
overshoots the `jal` and its delay slot. Nothing in between exists to write -
the call is a single statement, and there is no source position between "before
the call" and "after it returns".

Pinning is not the missing piece: `$19` is what GCC already chooses, so the
register is correct and only the placement is wrong.

### The same signature on three functions, and it is not a scheduling flag

This cluster, `func_80046294`'s jump-table cluster, and `func_800283F4`'s two
delay-slot words are the same shape: **an independent constant or address that
retail emits at the head of a block and GCC emits at the tail**, because GCC
orders by dependence and the constant has no consumer in the block.

Three functions failing identically suggests one cause rather than three, and
the obvious candidate is a scheduling-pass setting rather than three separate
source problems. That is testable, so it was tested: a probe profile adding
`-fno-schedule-insns` to each function's own profile measures **123** here
(three instructions long), **9** on `func_80046294` and **241** on
`func_800283F4` (two short). All three are worse, so the first scheduling pass
is not what separates them, and the probe profile was removed rather than
recorded.

The signature is therefore real and shared, but it is not reachable by turning a
pass off. It stays an open question rather than a closed one - worth another
attempt from the flag side only with a specific mechanism in mind, not by
sweeping.

### Closed on the loop rotation

Eight loop shapes were measured. `--i` in the condition costs 5, sinking the
decrement past the call costs 5, an inline assignment in the call argument is
two instructions long, and reordering the two stores costs 7. Four are inert
with the identical differing set: reordering `*slot`/`*ptr`, `i = i - 1` for
`i--`, a `for(;;)` with an explicit `break`, and the pointer-naming variants.
No profile beats 8 - four tie, and every other split profile is at least 96.

**Why the headline was wrong is worth recording.** Distance 0 is a strong claim:
it says the only thing left is order, which rules out an entire class of causes
and directs every subsequent attempt at scheduling and placement. Here the
build really does need one different instruction, so that direction was
mis-aimed. The `move`/`addu` identity is what makes this easy to get wrong -
counting mnemonics from a disassembly hides it, because the same encoding prints
two ways depending on whether the third operand is `$zero`.
