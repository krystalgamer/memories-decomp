## `func_8005D378` at 0x8005D378

`gcc_2_8_1_g8_split`, 395 instructions against a target of 391, **272
differing positions**, 361 of 391 aligning on opcode and registers. First
reconstruction; there was no stored candidate and the inventory note was empty.
This is **not** a near miss - it is 4 instructions long and recorded so the
semantics below are not re-derived.

**What the function is.** A keyframe interpolator for one animation node. It
takes the animation context, indexes a pointer array at `+0x14` by the context's
own `+0x14` field, and reads four slots from there: a track record and two
sample records, plus an optional publish pointer. The track's `+0x12` is the
duration and `+0x10` the elapsed time; a zero duration returns 1 immediately.

Every value is the same weighted average, `(from * t + to * (duration - t)) /
duration`, applied to nine quantities: the three translation components at
`+0x0`/`+0x2`/`+0x4` of each sample, the three angles at `+0x6`/`+0x8`/`+0xA`,
and the three scale components at `+0xC`/`+0xE`/`+0x10`.

The destination node is found by unpacking the track's first word: the top byte
indexes the same pointer array and the low 24 bits are a word index into it.
Translation lands at `+0x18`/`+0x1C`/`+0x20`, the interpolated angles at
`+0x44`/`+0x46`/`+0x48`, and `func_80089060` turns those into a matrix at
`+0x4`, which `ScaleMatrix` then scales. `+0x0` is cleared, and if the fourth
slot is non-null the three translation components are published to it.

**Angle wrapping, and it is the same idiom as `func_8004E7B0`.** When the
duration is exactly 16, each angle pair is put on the shortest path first: if
the two differ by `0x801` or more in either direction, the *destination* angle
is moved a full turn of `0x1000` toward the other, `+` when it is at or below
zero and `-` otherwise. Two details are easy to get backwards and both cost
instructions: it is the **`to`** angle that moves, not the `from` angle, and the
wrap is skipped when the duration is anything other than 16.

**Four structural findings so far.**

- *The two sample pointers are re-read from the slot array for every component,
  not cached in locals.* Caching them costs 15 instructions and 16 positions.
  The same applies to the publish pointer, which is re-read for each of its
  three stores.
- *The six angles, by contrast, are read into locals together* before any of
  them is used. That is worth 29 aligned instructions over reading them
  individually, so the rule is not "never cache" but "cache what retail loads in
  one run".
- *The publish stores read the low halfword of the translation*, which is a
  32-bit field, so they are `lhu` of `*(u16 *)&node->x` rather than a narrowing
  of the `s32`.
- *The three wrap bodies must stay separate.* Writing them with `goto` so they
  chain lets GCC cross-jump the identical tails and loses 11 more instructions;
  the `if`/`else if` form keeps them apart.

**What is left is 37 instructions**, mostly `nop`, `lw`, `lhu` and `sh`, which
points at more re-reads that this version still caches. The next pass should
look for values loaded once here and loaded per use in the target rather than
at register allocation, since at 37 short the shape is still wrong.


**367 to 273: the slot pointer has to be materialised, and parenthesisation is
what does it.**

The target computes the offset and then adds it to the context -
`addiu $v0,$v0,20` then `addu $s3,$a3,$v0` - and keeps the result as a base it
indexes from zero, `lw 0($s3)`, `lw 4($s3)`, `lw 8($s3)`, `lw 0xC($s3)`. The
first version wrote the same address as `(u8 *)ctx + (index << 2) + 0x14` and
GCC folded the `+ 0x14` into every load, so there was no slot pointer at all
and each access carried its own displacement.

Writing it `(u8 *)ctx + ((index << 2) + 0x14)` - the same value, one pair of
brackets - forces the offset to be formed first and the pointer to be
materialised. **That is worth 94 positions**, 367 to 273, and 28 aligned
instructions. `index * 4 + 0x14` and hoisting the offset into its own local are
identical to it.

It is worth being precise about why, because "add brackets" is not a lever.
The two expressions differ in *association*, and association decides whether
the constant joins the index or the base. Joined to the index it becomes part
of one offset that is computed once; joined to the base it is a separate
addend that GCC can sink into each load's displacement. Any candidate whose
target keeps a base pointer while the build repeats a displacement is worth
checking for this.

candidates of equal length. Both forms should be retried once the count is

**The publish block writes nine values, not three, and that was most of the
deficit.** The earlier description of this function said the fourth slot
receives the three translation components. It receives **nine**: translation
from `+0x18`/`+0x1C`/`+0x20`, the interpolated angles from
`+0x44`/`+0x46`/`+0x48`, and the three scale values from the stack temporaries,
written as halfwords to consecutive offsets `0` through `0x10`. Each of the
nine re-reads the slot pointer.

Correcting it takes the instruction deficit from **35 to 11** and the aligned
count from 322 to 346, at the same positional count. That is the second time on
this function that the positional count barely moved while the structure
improved a great deal, and it is the reason the entry is measured by deficit
and alignment rather than by the differing count alone.

The lesson for reading a tail like this is worth stating: nine
`lw`/`lhu`/`sh` triples with a constant stride look like an unrolled copy of
one array, and the temptation is to describe them by their first few and move
on. They are three *different* sources - a struct field, another struct field
sixteen words further on, and a stack temporary - feeding one contiguous
destination. The stride is in the destination only.

**The wrapped angle goes through a temporary, which is worth 15 aligned
instructions.** Retail sign-extends the destination angle into a register,
adjusts it there, and moves the result back:

explicit local recovers them: 346 to **361 aligned**, and the instruction count
from 11 short to 4 long.

**The goto form is still better by two measures and still merges.** Applying
the `bltz`/`beqz` structure on top of the temporary gives 266 differing and 364

stored because its count is nearest; whichever is right, the merging has to be
prevented before the goto form can be judged.

**What is left is small and specific.** Six `sra` in the target against six
`move` here, three `bltz` against three `bgez`, three `beqz` against three
`bnez`, and three `bgtz` extra. The six are the same sign extension: retail
extends the destination angle *twice*, once for the difference test and again
for the adjustment, where this version computes it once and reuses it. Nothing
in the source has yet stopped that CSE - the value does not change between the
two uses, so there is no dependence to break.

**A specific lead for that, from the shift instructions.** The target's sign
extensions are *not* uniform. There are exactly four **variable** shifts in the
whole function, `sllv`/`srav`, and they are the first angle's pair:

```
sllv $v0,$a2,$v1
srav $a0,$v0,$v1
sllv $v0,$a1,$v1
srav $v1,$v0,$v1
```

`$v1` at that point holds `0x10`, put there by the `bne $s2,$v1` that tests
whether the duration is 16. Every other sign extension in the function is a
constant `sll`/`sra` by 16.

A compiler does not choose a variable shift over a constant one for its own
reasons - a constant shift is always available and never worse. So the shift
amount was a *value* at that point in the original source and was
constant-propagated everywhere after the first pair. That is also exactly the
kind of thing that would stop the two sign extensions being CSE'd into one,
which is the residual this entry is stuck on.
