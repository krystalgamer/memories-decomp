## `func_80015EF4` at 0x80015EF4

`gcc_2_8_1_g0`, **386 of 386 instructions**, opcode multiset distance 20, 341
differing words. Two harness relocation bugs have moved this figure: a
`.rodata` jump-table relocation concealing a real difference took it from 339 to
340, and discarded REL addends took it from 340 to 341. The instruction count
and the distance are unchanged throughout. Written from scratch: no stored candidate, no rows in
`external_attempts.csv`, empty inventory note. The `rtps` / `c2` entries in an
opcode histogram cancel and are not part of that distance -- all four `rtps`
words are byte-identical to retail; objdump just names a bare `.word`
differently from splat.

The 3D card renderer. It immediately follows `display_projection.c` in the
image and shares its scratchpad projection idiom.

### What it does

Bails immediately unless bit `0x40` of the model's `+0x08` flags is set. Then it
seeds the rotation from the model's `+0x20`/`+0x21`/`+0x22` bytes scaled by 16,
adding `0x800` to the middle one when the holder's `+0x18` is at least `0xF`
(the half-turn that flips a card to the other player's facing), builds the
light matrix, and optionally scales it.

The quad is projected corner by corner through `func_80089CC0` into the four
`0x1F8003A0` slots, copied to `0x1F8003C0`, and then run through
`func_80087C70` four times, each writing a vertex pair into the primitive at
`prim+0x08`/`+0x04`, `+0x14`/`+0x10`, `+0x20`/`+0x1C`, `+0x2C`/`+0x28` and a
depth into its `0x1F8003E0` slot. If any of the four depths is negative the
whole card is off-screen and the function returns.

Shading comes from the model's `+0x5C`/`+0x5D` unless `func_800879A0` returns
non-positive, in which case it falls back to `0x38`/`0x80`; the second vertex
pair is always the first plus `0x34`/`0x3C`. The texture page word at
`prim+0x0E` is `((model->+0x42 + 0xF1) << 6) | 0x10`. Bits `0x60` of
`prim+0x1A` are replaced from bits 23..24 of the model's `+0x04` flags, and bit
`0x40000000` of the same word turns on semi-transparency and repaints the
sprite colour from the model's `+0x0C`/`+0x0D`/`+0x0E`.

Finally the mean of the four depths, `>> 4`, is the OT key for
`func_80084320`, and the four `0x1F8003C0` copies are projected with inline
`rtps` into the sprite's `+0x08`/`+0x10`/`+0x18`/`+0x20` before a second
`func_80084320` at a fixed key of `0xFFF`.

### Levers

- *The scratchpad pointers are initialised inside the early-exit guard.* Retail
  does the register saves, the flag test and the `beqz` first, and only then
  materialises the seven base pointers. Assigning them at the top of the
  function put eight instructions ahead of the branch and cost ten load-delay
  `nop`. This is the single largest lever here, 396 -> 386.
- *`0x1F800380` is an `SVECTOR`, not a `VECTOR`.* Retail writes it with three
  `sh`; a `VECTOR` gives three `sw`.
- *Base pointers, not absolute constants.* Writing every scratchpad address as
  an integer constant gives a `lui`/`ori` pair per reference and 72 `lui`
  against retail's 50. Typed pointer locals for `0x1F800300`, `0x1F8003A0`,
  `0x1F8003C0`, `0x1F800320` and `0x1F8003E0`, with the remaining references
  left as constants, reproduces retail's mix.
- *The `<< 4` on the model's `+0x21` really is computed twice.* Caching it in a
  local and reusing it for the `+ 0x800` arm is three instructions short of
  retail, which recomputes `lbu`/`sll` in the guarded arm. Verified both ways:
  386 with the recompute, 383 with the cache.
- *The `Blk8` char-struct assignment* is what produces the four
  `lwl`/`lwr`/`swl`/`swr` copies.

### GTE

Four `rtps`, written as one `__asm__ volatile` block with
`".word 0x4A180001"`, the same idiom the matched `display_projection.c`,
`func_8001B0CC.c`, `func_800177C4.c` and `func_800178BC.c` use, and for the
same reason: `gte_rtps()` in `src/psyq/inline_c.h` is `.word 0x0000007f`, an SN
marker word that assembles to the wrong four bytes under binutils.

**The sprite base must be passed as an operand, not named as a register.** An
earlier revision hardcoded `$21` because that is where retail holds it; GCC
places it in `$s6` here, so the block silently computed offsets from the
rotated-corner pointer instead and the build was wrong while still assembling
and still scoring 386 of 386. `: : "r"(sprite) :` with `%0` is correct and
costs nothing. The four scratchpad bases inside the block stay as `$8`-`$11`
with a clobber list, which is what retail uses.

### What is left

Distance 20, and the residual is concentrated rather than spread:

- **Six `nop` and three `lw`/four `sw`.** Retail issues more memory traffic and
  stalls less; the source order here leaves load-delay slots the scheduler
  cannot fill. Splitting the reused byte temporaries so two loads can be in
  flight -- the lever that pays on this kind of residual elsewhere -- makes it
  worse here (384, and 365 differing rather than 339), because retail really
  does load-then-store one pair at a time.
- **One inverted branch, and it guards dead code.** Retail emits
  `addu $v0, $zero, $zero` followed by `bnez $v0` around the `ScaleMatrix`
  block, i.e. a test on a literal zero that can never be taken, so the block
  always runs. That is not something a natural C form reproduces: any
  expression GCC can fold to zero it also folds the branch away, and testing
  the `func_80088C50` result gives a real `beqz` on `$v0`. **This is an open
  question and the most likely thing to unlock the rest** -- whatever
  construct retail used there is also what is shaping the surrounding
  scheduling. It is recorded here rather than guessed at.
