**What the shape needed.**

- *`D_8009B34E`, `D_8009B355`, `D_8009B344`, `D_8009B320` and
  `gDuel_wSelectedCardID` are declared as scalars in `.data`, not as
  arrays.* All five are one or two bytes and would otherwise be
  small-data at `-G8`, which is what `D_8009B1D5` and `D_8009B1C8` are:
  retail reaches those two through `$gp` and the other five through the
  assembler-macro `lui $at` / `%lo` form. Declared `extern u8 X[]`
  instead, GCC materialises one base register and shares it across all
  the accesses, which costs the `$at` form and two instructions.
- *The array base goes into a local before the index arithmetic.*
  `table = gDuel_adwCardStats;` then `table[value]` keeps the `-1` on
  the index (`addiu $v0, $a0, -0x1` / `sll` / `addu`); writing
  `gDuel_adwCardStats[id - 1]` lets `fold` distribute the multiply and
  the offset collapses into the load as `lw ..., -4(...)`.
- *The `>= 5` arm of the row test comes first.* Retail branches to the
  monster-row arm and falls through to the other one, so the source has
  to read `if (index % 15 >= 5) { ... } else { ... }`.
- *The unsigned compares are `>= 0 &&` conjuncts.* `sltiu $v0, $v0, 0x4`
  on the `pinned` byte and `sltiu` on the type digit are `fold` merging
  a `>= 0` test into the bound, not an unsigned variable.
- *One function-scope variable carries the `id - 1` index, the
  `Duel_CalcCardStats` result and the `index >= 15` flag.* Their live
  ranges are disjoint and retail allocates them to one register. A
  block-local variable for the call result is coalesced with `$v0` and
  the `addu $a1, $v0, $zero` copy disappears, which costs the
  instruction count; a second function-scope variable for the flag
  alone allocates the pair the other way round.
- *`rank` and `side` are block-local in each of the two rank blocks.*

**Three pins carry this, and the third is the interesting one.**
`register s32 rank asm("$2")` in both rank blocks and
`register s32 *dst asm("$4") = D_801D5608;` alongside each take the candidate
from 29 to 7. The neighbouring `func_80023090` in this same translation unit is
a matched function with four accepted pins, so a pinned finish is house-legal.

**The `id - 1` cluster is solved, and the solution is a lever worth reusing.**
Retail computes the index into `$v0`; every spelling that keeps one
function-scope `value` variable carrying the index, the `Duel_CalcCardStats`
result and the `index >= 15` flag puts it in `$a1` instead, because the variable
is allocated `$a1` for its whole range. Writing the subscript as a pure
expression frees the index, but then `value` moves *wholesale* from `$a1` to
`$a0` and six other positions break: the result copy, both shifts, the `li` of
the constant 2 and its two `sb` stores.

The constant is the key. `D_8009B34E = 2` appears twice in the row-test block,
so GCC keeps 2 in a register across both stores, and that CSE'd constant is what
takes `$a0` in retail and `$a1` in the candidate. Naming it and pinning it -
`register s32 two asm("$4") = 2;` at the top of the block, with both stores
written `D_8009B34E = two;` - puts the constant in `$a0`, leaves `$a1` for
`value`, and **removes all eight positions**: 7 to **5**.

**The lever: when two values are swapped between argument registers, pin the
one you were not looking at.** Pinning `value` itself to `$5` costs 14, because
retail wants it in `$a1` for two of its three roles and `$v0` for the third,
which one pin cannot express. Pinning the constant instead constrains the
*other* side of the swap and leaves the allocator free to do the right thing
with the variable that has the awkward lifetime. Pinning the constant to `$2`
instead costs 15, so it is the specific register that matters, not the act of
naming it.

**What is left is five positions, all of them the `D_801D5608` address**, in two
blocks with two different causes.

*Block 1, two positions: a clean swap of two independent instructions.* GCC's
own assembly, before MASPSX runs, emits

```
bne  $5,$2,$L12
lui  $4,%hi(D_801D5608)      <- delay slot, correct
addiu $4,$4,%lo(D_801D5608)  <- retail has `addu $18,$18,4` here
... the D_800E9FF0 chain ...
addu $18,$18,4               <- retail has the addiu here
beq  $2,$0,$L13
sw   $2,8($4)
```

so the `style += 4` update and the `%lo` completion trade places. This is
`sched2`: the `addiu` feeds the block's closing store and so has the longer path
to the block end, while `style` is not used again inside the block.
`-fno-schedule-insns2` is not available as a lever - it costs 193 positions.
Moving `style += 4` after the rank read, spelling it `style = style + 4`, and
moving it to the end of the block are neutral, neutral and +20.

The first two were re-measured at the *residual* rather than the count, because
an equal count does not by itself mean an equal residual. They are inert in the
stronger sense: reading the rank first, sinking the update below `dst[2] = rank`
and spelling it `style = style + 4` all leave the differing set at exactly
`{114, 122, 159, 164, 171}`, byte for byte the baseline's. So source order
within the block has no influence here at all, which is what `sched2` operating
on a dependence DAG predicts - the two instructions are independent, and their
order in the RTL stream before scheduling does not survive into it. This closes
source ordering as an approach to block 1 rather than leaving it as an
untested-by-window "neutral".

Deferring the pointer in *this* block the way that helps block 2 - declaring
`dst` uninitialised and assigning it after `style += 4` - costs 6 (5 to 11).

*Block 2, three positions: retail's first delay slot is empty.* Retail leaves
the `beqz` slot at +0x27C empty, spends the *next* branch delay slot on
`lui a0,%hi`, and the load-delay slot after `lb v0,25(v0)` on
`addiu a0,a0,%lo`; the candidate does each one step sooner. The cause is that
the initialised declaration makes the `lui` the first instruction of the
fall-through block, so GCC's delay-slot pass takes it; the only other candidate,
`lb $2,16($19)`, is excluded because it redefines the branch's register.
Assigning the pointer after the `y` test **does** produce retail's empty slot,
but costs eight elsewhere and only recovers to 8 with `side` pinned to `$5`.
Dropping the pointer for the array name in either block costs 11.

The obvious remaining idea was that the pointer only had to move *later within
the same block*, not past the `y` test: retail's order is `lb y`, `lbu side`,
then the address, so writing the assignment after the side read looked like it
should reproduce it without paying the join-block cost. It does not. Assigning
`dst` after `side = D_8009B1D5;`, comma-sequencing the two, and additionally
reading `y` into a local first all leave the differing set at exactly
`{114, 122, 159, 164, 171}`. GCC materialises the address at block entry
wherever in the block it is written, so `dbr` still finds the `lui` at the head
of the fall-through block and still fills the slot retail leaves empty.

**Both clusters are therefore the same negative, and it is a CFG one.** Six
placements across the two blocks - three in each - give a byte-identical
residual; the only changes that move anything at all are the two that cross a
basic-block boundary, and both are worse (+8 past the join, +6 out of the
block). Intra-block statement order is not a weak handle on either cluster, it
is not a handle, and it cannot contribute partial credit to a combination
either. Anything that reaches these five positions has to change the block
structure or come from outside the source.

### The identical-arm conditional does not reach it either

`func_80045208` was matched by a lever that did not exist when the placement
sections above were written: an identical-arm conditional,
`first = table ? *table : *table`, which defeats the scheduler rule that sinks a
load-fed store to the end of its block. It is worth testing here because both
residues are `sched2` choosing between two ready instructions, and because that
lever's known precondition - the values scoped to an inner block - is already
satisfied in both of these blocks.

It does not apply, and the reason is specific rather than circumstantial. The
lever works because `operand_equal_p` refuses to call two memory dereferences
equal, so `fold` leaves the conditional standing and the value keeps a
dependence it would not otherwise have. An address constant is folded on sight.
Three spellings chosen so the arms differ syntactically while denoting the same
address - `style ? D_801D5608 : &D_801D5608[0]`, its mirror, and
`&D_801D5608[0]` alone - are all inert at 5.

So this is a negative about the *arms*, not about the scope, and it is the one
new lever since these clusters were closed. The reading above stands: reaching
these five positions needs the block structure to change or something from
outside the source.

**The address cluster does not respond to the constant lever.** The trick that
solved the `id - 1` cluster - constrain the other side of the swap - was tried

This is the distinction that matters: the `id - 1` cluster was an *allocation*

remains is *placement* - which of two ready instructions `sched2` issues first,

**The additive CSE lever does not reach this residual, in its precise form.**
The rule extracted from `func_8005C1F4` is that repeating an expression

entry above already recorded that mixing array-name and pointer spellings for

That is a useful boundary for the new rule rather than only a negative here.
The additive lever acts on a redundant **load**, which CSE turns into a copy;
this residual is the *placement* of an address materialisation whose
instructions are all present already - the opcode multiset is identical. There
is no redundant load to introduce, so there is nothing for CSE to collapse.

**Block 1 read correctly at last: it is a load-delay slot, not a plain swap.**
The earlier description of block 1 as two independent instructions trading
places is right but understates what decides it. Retail's `%lo` completion at
`+0x1E8` sits in the *load-delay slot* of `lb $v0,25($v0)` at `+0x1E4`, whose
result the `beqz` at `+0x1EC` needs immediately. Both `addiu`s are legal
fillers for that slot; the candidate spends it on `style += 4` and completes
the `%lo` early, retail does the reverse. So the question is not which of two
instructions is scheduled first in the abstract but which one the scheduler
keeps back to fill a hazard.
