## `Duel_LoadPackageStage` at 0x800171A8

`gcc_2_8_1_g8_split`, 235 instructions against a target of 237, **two
instructions short**, both at one cross-jumping boundary. This improves the
previous stored candidate (242 instructions, opcode distance 7); there are no
rows for this address in `external_attempts.csv`.

The thirteen-stage callback registered with `File_RequestAsyncTransfer` by
`func_8001798C` and `func_800179F4` for the 235-sector duel terrain package.
Each stage fills the transfer descriptor for the next chunk: stages 1, 5, 7, 8,
9 and 11 point it at a fixed buffer and set the size, stages 3 to 5 hand over
the equip, fusion and ritual tables, stages 2 and 6 push a VRAM strip through
`LoadImage2` first, and stages 0, 6, 10 and 12 also set the destination
rectangle and mark the descriptor as an image stage. Every stage clears one of
`0x20000` / `0x30000` out of `D_8009B0F4` and writes 1 or 2 to `done`.

Integration will need three `split.yaml` rodata lines, because the file owns
`jtbl_800100C0` at file offset 0x8C0: `initial_data_0a` has to be cut at 0x8C0,
`[0x8C0, .rodata, game/<file>]` added, and the trailing alignment word at 0x8F4
left to a new `initial_data_0a?` segment - the pad after the last table belongs
to the following segment, not to this file.

The descriptor prerequisite is shared in `file_transfer.h`: neutral `u16`
fields `field_04`, `field_06`, and `field_32` occupy the original padding at
`+0x04`, `+0x06`, and `+0x32`. Retail stores at `0x80017200`, `0x80017210`,
and `0x800171F8` confirm the halfword accesses; stages 6, 10, and 12 corroborate
them. Compile-time assertions protect the `0x48` size and the exposed/adjacent
offsets. No matching consumer names the changed padding ranges, and a private
header patch is no longer needed. The names deliberately do not assert
image-specific meanings for other transfer users.

### What is left: one `addiu`, one `sw`, and a single register

Against the target the opcode multiset differs in exactly two entries,
`addiu` -1 and `sw` -1. Every other opcode count is equal, so the deficit is
one specific merge rather than anything spread through the body.

**The merge.** In the build, stages 0 and 10 *and* the image tail all end with
the same three instructions, so GCC cross-jumps all three onto one label:

```
sw    $2,8($17)
addu  $2,$2,2048
sw    $2,12($17)
```

Retail merges only stages 0 and 10, onto a four-instruction block that also
carries the mode store, and leaves the image tail with its own copy.

The arithmetic confirms this is the whole deficit. Folding the image tail in
saves its three tail instructions but forces the `sw mode` out of the shared
block and into a delay slot in each of stages 0 and 10, so two `sw` come back:
net one `sw` and one `addiu` short, which is exactly the multiset difference.

**Retail is kept apart by a register, not by a shape.** Cross-jumping compares
instructions including their hard registers. Retail's image tail holds the
`D_8009B118` pointer in `$v1`:

```
lui/lw $v1, D_8009B118
addiu  $v0,0x10 ; sh $v0,0x6($s1)
sw     $v1,0x8($s1) ; addiu $v1,$v1,0x800 ; sw $v1,0xC($s1)
```

while stages 0 and 10 hold it in `$v0`. The two tails are therefore not
identical and cannot merge. The build puts it in `$v0` in all three. **The
instruction order already matches retail exactly; only the register differs.**

**Where that register is decided.** Retail reads the volatile `D_8009B0F4`
*after* the counter and `field_32` stores, so `$v0` is free at the read and
takes `flags`, which leaves `$v1` for the `0x40` that `field_04` stores and,
later, for the pointer. The build hoists that read to immediately after the
mask setup, so `flags` takes `$v1` and `$v0` cycles through the constants and
then the pointer.

That hoist is **not reachable from statement order**: moving
`flags = D_8009B0F4 & 0xFFDDFFFF;` above `d->field_04 = 0x40;` in both arms
produces a byte-identical arm.

### What variable reuse can and cannot do

Making the mask and the image stage's `0x10000` the **same** C variable puts
`0x10000` into `$a0`, which is exactly where retail has it, and is the first
probe to move that constant at all.

The same trick is inert on `d->done = 2` and `d->field_06 = 0x10`. Writing both
through one reassigned variable changes nothing, because those are cheap
immediates and GCC propagates them back into the stores. **Variable reuse
controls register sharing only for constants expensive enough to survive as a
value** - here the two-instruction `lui`/`ori` mask. That is the useful
generalisation, and it says the pointer/constant pair in the image tail cannot
be steered this way either.

### The only shape that splits the tails

Giving stages 0 and 10 an explicit shared tail - locals `p` and `mode` with a
`goto` to a label placed after stage 10 - is the one probe that produces two
tails. It costs seven instructions. A `mode` local's constant is hoisted to the
top of its arm, which leaves the two arms identical for eight instructions, and
GCC cross-jumps those as well.

That failure is informative: retail depends on the mode constant being the
*last* instruction before the shared block, `lui` for `0x20000` against `addiu`
for `0x4000`, so the backward scan stops immediately. Any reconstruction that
lets the mode constant float away from the label loses that boundary.

### Crossed without improving on two short

Reusing `flags` for the second read-modify-write instead of `|=`; reading the
flag word before the `field_04` store in both image arms; both of those
combined; a block-scoped variable for the image `0x10000`; the mask/`0x10000`
shared variable; loading the pointer into a local declared before `done = 2`,
with and without the shared mask; and one reassigned variable for `done` and
`field_06`, with and without it. All eleven give 235 instructions.

Profiles: the eleven non-`-msplit-addresses` profiles give -3 at 181 differing;
all six split profiles give -2; disabling either scheduling pass overshoots, by
+5 for `g0_no_sched2_split` up to +33 for `g0_no_sched1`.

Everything else lines up. The other shared tail, the one stages 6 and 12 use,
took three tries to place: written as duplicated straight-line code the build
came out three instructions long, and written as a `goto` with the whole tail
shared it came out five short. The stored source is the middle form: each arm
computes `flags = D_8009B0F4 & 0xFFDDFFFF;` and jumps to a label that starts at
the `field_04` store, which is exactly where retail's `.L800174F8` begins.

Tried for stage 10 and rejected: the same `goto` treatment for stages 0 and 10
(228, gcc then merges the whole `|= 0x10000` sequence as well), hoisting the
`D_8009B118` read into each arm (230), and six statement orders inside the two
stages.

### Levers that got it here

- **`D_8009B0F4` is `volatile` and needs the `.data` attribute.** Volatile is
  what keeps every read-modify-write separate, as retail has them; the section
  attribute is what keeps it out of small data at `-G8` so each access gets its
  own `lui`, which is the assembler macro form retail uses. A plain extern comes
  out `%gp_rel` and the function is twenty instructions short.
- **`-msplit-addresses` is required.** The jump-table dispatch is `lui`,
  `addiu`, `sll`, `addu`, `lw` - five instructions. Without the flag the
  assembler's `$at` macro does it in four.
- **`d->value_08 = d->value_0C = x;` is the right chaining order.** Retail
  stores `+0xC` first; the other order swaps them.
- **A store between two volatile accesses stays between them.** Stage 10's
  `field_32 = 0` sits inside the `|= 0x10000` read-modify-write in retail, and
  writing it there in the source is what puts it there in the build.
