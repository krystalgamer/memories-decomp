## `func_80048768` at 0x80048768

`gcc_2_8_1_cc_g0_as_g8`, 110 instructions against a target of 110, opcode
distance 2, **22 differing positions**.

*The stored source now carries the four pins.* The entry below already recorded
that the `key`/`hi`/`lo`/`near` subset reaches 28, but the source block kept was
the unpinned one at 56, so half the entry's own best result was not in the file
anyone would pick up. Both were rebuilt to confirm before swapping: unpinned 56,
four pins 28, all seven 37. Supersedes the 111/110 distance-3 entry below it, and its
"exact-length alternative" at distance 4, both of which are kept for their
reading of the function.

A sound-driver pan update. The first argument is a key: bit 0x8000 forwards
straight to `func_80044E90` with the pan sign-extended, and the
`0xF000 == 0x4000` form is an indirect key resolved through two tables hanging
off `g_SDValue` before the main pass. Any lookup yielding `0xFFFF` abandons the
call. The main pass walks the four voices, reads each envelope through
`SpuGetVoiceEnvelope`, and for the voice whose key at +0x404 matches, scales the
sample's base level at +0x444 by the distance either side of centre and stores
the two channel levels at +0x414 and +0x41C.

**The two residuals the old entry listed were one problem, and it is the loop
form.** The old entry recorded them separately: the far-window test being
hoisted out of the loop, and the parameter copies not being emitted at the top
of the function. They are the same thing. `(u16)(arg1 + 0x80) < 0x80` is
loop-invariant, so GCC's loop optimiser lifts it and `arg1` dies before the
loop, which is exactly why no copy of it into a callee-saved register is
needed. Retail keeps `arg1` in `$s6` across the whole function and recomputes
the window test on every iteration, three instructions inside the `pan != 0`
arm.

Writing the body as a `goto` loop fixes both at once: GCC 2.8's loop optimiser
only sees loops the front end marked, so nothing is hoisted, `arg1` stays live,
and the `addu $s6, $a1, $zero` prologue copy appears where retail has it.
Assigning the test to a local inside the arm, which the old entry tried, does
not do it - the invariant is still an invariant.

**The `0x100` term has three reachable spellings and none of them is
retail's.** Retail computes it `andi $v1, $a0, 0x100` / `sltu $v1, $zero, $v1`
/ `sll $v1, $v1, 6`. The measured spellings:

| source | emitted | count |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `((u32)(x & 0x100) > 0) << 6` inline | `andi`, `beqz`, `nop`, `addiu 64` | 4 |
| the same assigned to a local first | `srl 2`, `andi 0x40` | 2 |
| `((x & 0x100) >> 8) << 6` | `andi`, `sra 8`, `sll 6` | 3 |

The third is what the stored source uses: it has retail's instruction count and
its `andi`/shift/`sll` shape, and is one opcode away on the middle instruction.
The reason none of them reaches `sltu` is combine: `(ne (and x pow2) 0)` used
as a 0/1 value is rewritten into a shift whenever the single bit is provable,
and against a literal mask it always is. Also measured and folded the same way:
`* 0x40` instead of `<< 6`, `?:`, `|` instead of `+`, `!!`, `>= 1`, `> 0u`,
`(u16)x & 0x100`, `((u32)x >> 8) & 1`, `(x & 0x100) >> 2`, the boolean as
`u8`/`u32`/`s16`, shifting it in its own statement, folding the term into an
index scaled by two, and reusing `near`, `lo`, `hi` and `v` as the boolean.

### The four pins are the stored form, and a fifth does not pay

Re-measured from the entry's own numbers, which hold exactly: the unpinned
source gives 56, the `key`/`hi`/`lo`/`near` subset 28, and all seven pins 37.
The 28-position form is now the stored source, since there is no reason for the
file to carry a version 28 positions worse than the one the entry describes.

Extending the subset does not help. Added to the four:

| fifth pin | differing |
| --- | --- |
| `i` to `$s0` | 28, no change |
| `pan` to `$s1` | 29 |
| `pan2` to `$s6` | 35 |

`i` is the allocno the entry identifies as retail's first, and pinning it to
retail's register is **inert** - not worse, but not a gain either. That is the
second of the two pin failure modes recorded on `func_80031874`: the register
assignment for `i` is already what retail has once the other four are fixed, so
there is nothing for the pin to correct. The remaining 28 positions are
elsewhere.

**Pinning the looked-up key is worse everywhere, which is a third outcome
again.** Three of the remaining positions are the value loaded at `+0x44C`:
the candidate holds it in `$a1` and retail in `$v0`. It is already a named
local, so unlike the far store's base it needs no naming step - the pin can be
applied directly.

Every register measures **27**, five worse than the unpinned 22, including
retail's own `$v0`.

So this function has now shown all three ways a pin can land, and they are
distinguishable by their signature rather than by guesswork:

| signature | meaning | example here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| retail's register improves, others are worse | the assignment was wrong and the pin fixes it | the multiply product, 26 to 22 |
| retail's register is neither better nor worse | the value is not what decides; the difference is a knock-on | the far store's base, 22 either way |
| **every** register including retail's is worse | the value's placement is load-bearing for something else | the looked-up key, 27 everywhere |

The third case is the one that reads most like a mistake and is not. Binding
`v` to any fixed register costs the allocator freedom it was using elsewhere,
so the five positions are lost away from the three the pin was aimed at. Where
the second signature says *stop, wrong value*, the third says *stop, this value
is already doing work* - and neither is worth re-testing when the base moves,
unlike the first.

**The far store's base is not reachable by naming it.** Six of the remaining
positions are the `g_SDValue` base of the second level store: the candidate
holds it in `$a2` and retail in `$v1`, with the index register shifted to match
(`$a3` against `$a2`). Since naming and pinning the multiply product had just
paid four positions, the same treatment of this base is the obvious next move.

It does nothing. Binding the read to a `base2` local is **inert** at 22, and
pinning that local measures `$3` at 22 - retail's own register, no change - with
`$6`, `$7`, `$8` and `$9` all at 38.

That a pin on retail's register is neither better nor worse is the signature of
the value not being the one that decides. The `$a2`/`$v1` split is a knock-on
from the allocation above it rather than a choice about this read, which is why
constraining the read cannot move it. Worth recording next to the product pin
that did work: the two look like the same shape in the diff - a base register
holding the wrong name - and only one of them is a value the source can
constrain.

### 26 to 22: naming the two multiply products

The two level stores end `mflo $t0` / `sh $t0` in the candidate and
`mflo $v0` / `sh $v0` in retail. The product is an anonymous temporary, so the
route is the one recorded on `func_8005C1F4`: give it a name so it can be
pinned.

Naming it alone is inert - a single `prod` local holding both products, written
as its own statement before each store, measures the same **26** with an
identical position set. Pinning that local is what pays:

| pin | differing |
| --- | --- |
| `$2` | **22** |
| `$8`, `$12`, `$24`, `$25` | 30 |
| unpinned | 26 |

So this is the first failure mode from `func_80031874` rather than the second:
the naming was free, and the register assignment was wrong until the pin fixed
it. Both products share one local, which is what lets a single pin cover two
stores - they are in mutually exclusive arms, so their live ranges never
overlap and GCC is content to give them the same register.

The instruction count and the opcode multiset are unchanged at 110 and
distance 2, so the four positions are pure register naming, which is what a pin
is for.

### The `off` sum's operand order is worth two positions

The two terms of `off` are summed the other way round in retail. Writing the
`0x100` term first,

```c

extern void func_80044E90(s32);
extern void SpuGetVoiceEnvelope(s32, void *);
extern void func_80047864(s32);

void func_80048768(s32 arg0, s32 arg1)
{
    s32 i;
    s32 pan;
    register s32 lo asm("$20");
    register s32 hi asm("$19");
    register s32 near asm("$21");
    register s32 key asm("$18");
    s16 env[4];
    u8 *base;
    s32 v;
    s32 off;
    s32 pan2;
    register s32 prod asm("$2");

    key = arg0;
    pan2 = arg1;
    if (arg0 & 0x8000) {
        func_80044E90((s16)arg1);
        return;
    }
    i = 0;
    if ((arg0 & 0xF000) == 0x4000) {
        off = (((arg0 & 0x100) >> 8) << 6) + ((arg0 & 0x1F) * 2);
        base = g_SDValue;
        v = *(u16 *)(base + off + 0x44C);
        if (v == 0xFFFF) {
            return;
        }
        key = *(u16 *)(*(u8 **)(base + 0x43C) + v * 2);
        if (key == 0xFFFF) {
            return;
        }
    }
    near = (u16)(arg1 - 1) < 0x80;
    pan = (s16)arg1;
    lo = 0x80 - pan;
    hi = pan + 0x80;

    {
        SpuGetVoiceEnvelope(i + 0x14, env);
        base = g_SDValue;
        v = *(u16 *)(base + i * 2 + 0x404);

            if (env[0] != 0) {
                if (pan != 0) {
                    if (near) {
                        prod = *(u8 *)(*(u8 **)(base + 0x444) + v * 8) * lo;
                        *(u16 *)(base + i * 2 + 0x414) = prod;
                    }
                    if ((u16)(pan2 + 0x80) < 0x80) {
                        prod = *(u8 *)(*(u8 **)(g_SDValue + 0x444) + v * 8) * hi;
                        *(u16 *)(g_SDValue + i * 2 + 0x41C) = prod;
                    }
                }
                func_80047864(i);
            }
        }
        i++;
    }
    if (i < 4) {
        goto loop;
    }

```

takes the residual from **28 to 26**: the closing `addu`'s operand order comes
out retail's way. This is the operand-order class that `(s32)` casts and
shift-spelt subscripts reach on other functions, and it is worth noting that
here it is the *addition's* argument order that carries it, not the spelling of
either term - `<< 1` for the low term and `|` instead of `+` for the join are
both byte-identical at 28.

Naming the two terms as locals and summing them in the same order reaches the
same 26 with an **identical position set**, so the inline form is kept: two
extra locals that buy nothing are two more things for the next reader to
account for.

What the swap does not fix is which register each term lands in. Retail
computes the low term into `$v0` and the high term into `$v1`; both orderings
of the source put them the other way round. That half is downstream of the
`sltu` the high term cannot be spelled into - the entry's table of three
reachable spellings for the `0x100` value still stands - so the remaining
positions in this block are held by the same distance-2 blocker rather than by
the sum.

**Address sums do not respond to the same swap.** The `off` result above
invites trying operand order on the three subscript expressions, where retail
also differs: it computes `base + i * 2` base-first at `+0x0E0` and `+0x16C`
while the candidate emits the index first. Writing the subscripts index-first
in the source - `i * 2 + base + 0x404` and the two like it, singly and all
three together - is **byte-identical** at 26 in every case.

So the lever is narrower than "operand order of an addition". It reaches a sum
of two *values* being computed into a register, and not a sum forming an
*address*, because address arithmetic is canonicalised before the operand order
of the source survives into it. That distinction is worth carrying to other
candidates: the two look identical in a diff, both appearing as an `addu` with
its arguments the wrong way round, and only one of them is reachable.

**Retail shares `0x80` in a register, and the source cannot ask for that.**
At `+0x0BC` retail materialises `li $v0,128` and then spends it twice -
`subu $s4,$v0,$s1` for `lo` and `addu $s3,$s1,$v0` for `hi`. The candidate
materialises it into `$s4` and reaches for an immediate on the second use,
`addiu $s3,$s1,128`.

That is the shape the additive-CSE lever in `matching-evidence.md` is for:
write the shared thing a second time and let CSE collapse it. It does not
apply, and the reason is the boundary that lever already states. Binding the
constant to a local used by both - `k = 0x80; lo = k - pan; hi = pan + k;` - is
**byte-identical** at 26, as is writing `hi` with the constant first. GCC
propagates the constant back to both sites before CSE ever sees a shared value.

The additive lever acts on a redundant **load**, which CSE turns into a
register copy. A constant has no load to make redundant, so there is nothing to
collapse and nothing to share. That now holds on four functions - the channel
index in `func_800283F4`, the loop-invariant address in `func_80012E5C`, the
scratchpad pointer in `func_80031874`, and this one - and it is the same rule
seen from the other side each time: **a constant is placed and duplicated by
the compiler alone, so neither its position nor its sharing is a source
lever.**

### What the register difference actually is

The entry says retail keeps `arg1` in `$s6` across the function, and the
prologue confirms it: `move $s6,$a1` at `+0x010` is present in both builds,
byte for byte. But that is not where the candidate diverges.

Retail keeps reading `$a1` **as well** for the uses near the top of the
function - `sll $a0,$a1,0x10` at `+0x034`, `addiu $v0,$a1,-1` at `+0x0A8`,
`sll $v0,$a1,0x10` at `+0x0B4` - and reaches for `$s6` only later. The
candidate reads `$s6` at all three. So the copy is not the difference; the
difference is that retail's original argument register stays in use after the
copy is made and the candidate's does not.

That is a sharper statement than "retail keeps `arg1` in `$s6`", and it rules
out the obvious response. Making the copy later so the early reads see the
parameter costs positions rather than saving them:

| placement of `pan2 = arg1` | differing |
| --- | --- |
| before the `0x8000` early return (stored) | **28** |
| after it, before the near test | 60 |
| after it, before `i = 0` | 43 |

Both later placements do move some early reads onto `$a1`, and both lose more
elsewhere than they gain. Combined with the earlier finding that `arg1` and
`pan2` are interchangeable in the window tests - byte-identical either way,
because CSE collapses them before allocation - the source has no way to say
"copy this parameter but keep using the original register for the next few
reads". The copy is either visible to CSE from the top, which is the stored
form, or it is late, which costs more than it fixes.

**What is left is a permutation of the seven callee-saved registers.** The
frame is retail's `0x38` and the same seven registers are saved in the same
slots; the mapping differs:

```
retail   i->$s0  pan->$s1  key->$s2  hi->$s3  lo->$s4  near->$s5  pan2->$s6
build    key->$s0  i->$s1  pan->$s2  pan2->$s3  hi->$s4  lo->$s5  near->$s6
```

Declaration order is inert - twenty-six orders all score 56. Pins move it but
do not close it: the best four-pin subset (`key`, `hi`, `lo`, `near`) reaches
28 positions, `key` plus `pan2` alone reaches 29, and pinning all seven is
*worse* at 37 and distance 4, because the caller-saved temporaries in the
key-resolution block then move instead. The likely cause is that the `goto`
loop, which is needed for the shape, also removes the loop weighting from
`i`'s references, and `i` is exactly the allocno retail ranks first. That is
the tension to attack next: some way to stop the hoist inside a loop the front
end has marked.

### Both window tests are already where they should be

The entry says retail recomputes the window test on every iteration rather than
hoisting it, which reads as an instruction to move the source's hoisted `near`
into the loop. Measured, that is backwards for this candidate:

| change | instructions | differing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| stored source | 110 | 56 |
| `near` recomputed inline in the arm | 110 | 78 |
| the far test hoisted to a local beside `near` | 109 | 107 |

So the split the stored source already has - `near` hoisted, the far test
inline - is the right one, and both departures from it are worse. The entry's
observation about recomputation describes what the `goto` loop achieves for the
*far* test; it is not a further change to make.

**`arg1` and `pan2` are the same value to GCC.** Both tests are insensitive to
which of the two they read: writing the far test against `arg1` instead of
`pan2`, and the near test against `pan2` instead of `arg1`, are each
**byte-identical**, position set included. The second parameter copy is
therefore not buying a separately allocated live value, which is worth knowing
because the entry's account of the residual rests on retail holding `arg1` in
`$s6` across the function. Whatever distinguishes retail's register map, it is
not that these two reads are kept apart - the source cannot keep them apart,
because CSE collapses them before allocation sees them.

That narrows the remaining seven-register permutation: it cannot be attacked by
splitting the parameter into two live values, which was the most obvious
reading of "retail keeps `arg1` in `$s6`".

## What moved it from distance 5

**Both parameters are copied to saved registers as the first thing the function
does.** The target interleaves `$s2 = $a0` and `$s6 = $a1` with the register
saves, before the 0x8000 test. Assigning them only where they are first needed
emits the copies in the middle of the prologue and costs three positions.

**The near test is an unsigned window, and so is the far test.**
`(u16)(pan - 1) < 0x80` and `(u16)(pan + 0x80) < 0x80` give the `andi 0xFFFF`
plus `sltiu 0x80` pairs; signed range tests produce compares.

## What is left

Three opcodes and eleven positions, in two places.

**The `0x100` term still if-converts.** The target computes it branchlessly as
`andi`, `sltu $v1, $zero, $v1`, `sll 6`, then adds it to the halfword index and
uses one constant `0x44C` displacement. Every spelling tried so far lets GCC
fold the `+0x40` into the load displacement and select between `0x44C` and
`0x48C` with a branch instead: writing the term as `!= 0`, as
`(u32)(x & 0x100) > 0`, combining with `|` rather than `+`, hoisting the bit
into its own variable used twice, and forming the base pointer before the
displacement. The `> 0` spelling is kept here because it wins elsewhere.

**The far window test is hoisted.** The target evaluates it inside the
`pan != 0` arm on every iteration, two instructions; this build lifts three
instructions out of the loop. Assigning it to a local inside that arm does not
prevent the hoist.
