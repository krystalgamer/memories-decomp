## `func_8001944C` at 0x8001944C

`gcc_2_8_1_g8_split`, 70 of 70 instructions, opcode distance 0, 15 differing
positions, and identical at 15 under all six split profiles.

The card-image refresh. It waits out the GPU, picks a VRAM x offset from
`gGraphics_bActiveBuffer`, reads the 0x8C by 0xC4 window at the object's `x`
and `y` (offsets 0x30 and 0x32, the same `Obj` layout the matched neighbour
`func_80019564` uses) into `D_8015C424` with `StoreImage2`, sets the
semi-transparency bit on all 0x6B30 halfwords, clears eleven specific
halfwords, and writes the block back to a fixed VRAM slot with `LoadImage2`.

Six canonical attempts, no post-terminal work. The instruction multiset is
exact and every remaining difference is a register name or one scheduling
swap; nothing structural is left.

**What the shape needed.**

- *The far clears go through a local at `+0x8000`.* Six of the eleven are at
  byte offsets 0xD430 to 0xD65E. Written as offsets from the array, GCC folds
  each into a `symbol + offset` relocation and addresses them off a base it
  picks itself. Written as `q = buf + 0x8000` with the remainder at the use,
  it emits `ori $v0, $zero, 0x8000`, one `addu`, and 0x5430-style
  displacements, which is the target. This is the same split lever recorded
  for `func_8005BE3C`, at a different boundary.
- *The near clears need a pointer variable, not the array name.* Through the
  name, GCC emits each store as an absolute `sh zero, sym+off` macro, which
  the assembler expands to a `lui` plus the store, two instructions each.
- *The buffer must stay live to the last statement.* The final
  `*(u16 *)buf = 0` before `LoadImage2` is what keeps `buf` alive past the
  bit-setting loop, and that is the only reason the loop gets its own walking
  pointer (`addu $a1, $s0, $zero`) instead of walking the callee-saved
  register directly. Spell that last clear through the array name or through
  the second base and the build is one instruction short. Worth knowing
  generally: a store that looks redundant at the end of a function can be the
  thing that forces a copy earlier.
- *Branch polarity.* `xoff = 0; if (flag == 0) xoff = 0x140;` gives the
  target's `bnez` with the zero in the delay slot; the `if/else` spelling
  inverts it.
- *The last clear sits between the width and height stores*, not after them.
  Worth 7 positions on its own.

**What is left** is three clusters of register naming plus one scheduling
swap, on an exact multiset:

- the loop's walking pointer and counter come out `$v1`/`$a0` where the target
  has `$a1`/`$a2`. Both are copies of the same callee-saved register, and
  `$v1` is simply earlier in the allocation order; the target reuses the
  argument register the call had just finished with.
- `lui $v0` then `addiu $s0, $v0` where the target coalesces both into `$s0`.
- after the loop the two address materialisations are emitted in the opposite
  order, `D_800E9D70` before `D_8015C424`.

Crossed without improving on 15: all eight assignments of which of the two
buffer references feeds the near clears, the final clear and the `LoadImage2`
argument; three placements of the final clear within the rect stores; the
rect block before and after the clears; sixteen declaration orders; the loop
pointer as the `StoreImage2` argument; the loop walked as `u8 *` with a `u16`
cast; the loop indexed so GCC builds the induction variable itself (one
instruction short); and all profiles.
