## `func_80031874` at 0x80031874

`gcc_2_8_1_g8_split_no_strength_reduce`, 280 of 280 instructions, **opcode
distance 0**, **16 differing positions**.

*Headline corrected on recovery.* This entry's body was developed from 68 down
to 43 and the source block below is the 43-position one, but the headline was
never updated to match, and the whole entry sat on an unmerged branch while
`master` kept the 68-position version. Both are re-measured here: the source on
`master` gives 68, the source below gives 43. Written from scratch; there was no
previous candidate and no external attempt rows.

Draws the eight rows of a card list into the ordering table. It builds two
sprites in the scratchpad - the row sprite at `0x1F800020` and the header tag
at `0x1F800060` - and encodes every number through the shared text buffer at
`0x1F800000`. The list's record block is `D_8009B2FC + kind * 0x2D4C + 4`,
where the kind comes from the object's `+0x67`; `+0x2D3C` is the scroll offset
that selects the first of the sixteen-byte rows and `+0x2D45` is the header
argument to `func_80031784`.

## Four things the shape needed

**The profile is `no_strength_reduce`, and it is the only one that reaches the
instruction count.** Every other profile is seven instructions long. Seven
matched functions already use it, so it is not an outlier here.

**`obj->field_4` is read once into a local.** It is stored into both sprites;
reading it twice costs an instruction and a load-delay `nop`.

**The record block's `+4` belongs to the offset, not the base.** Written
`D_8009B2FC + (kind * 0x2D4C + 4)` GCC emits `sll` / `lw` of the pointer /
`addiu 4` / `addu`, which is retail's order; written
`D_8009B2FC + kind * 0x2D4C + 4` the `addiu` moves after the `addu`.

**The three byte-table reads keep the base first.** `*(D_8009B2FC + value +
0x606A)` gives `addu $v0, $v0, $s2` with the pointer first;
`D_8009B2FC[value + 0x606A]` groups the index with the constant and comes out
`addu $v0, $s2, $v0`. Three positions, and the same `expand_expr` association
rule recorded for `func_8004C114` and #2073.

## What is left is entirely the prologue

Every one of the 68 differing positions is below `0x118`. The whole eight-row
loop - all four `func_800316F0` calls, the two `Text_EncodeDecimalDigits`
pairs, the `GsSortFastSprite` calls, the colour logic and the loop tail -
matches exactly, and the opcode multiset over the whole function is identical.

The residual is one phase shift plus register naming. This build materialises
the `0x1F800000` text pointer at `0x0090`; retail materialises it at `0x00e8`,
immediately before it is reused as the high half of the `0x1F800060` argument
(`addu $a0, $s3, $zero` / `ori $a0, $a0, 0x60`). Everything between drifts by
one slot behind that. Nothing tried moves it: four placements of the
`text` assignment including inside the branch that first uses it, three
declaration orders for the three scratchpad pointers, four orderings of the
viewport subtractions against the `0x290`/`0xFA` stores, and separate locals
for the two viewport reads.

**Three changes take it to 46 positions with the loop body still exact, and
none of them works without the other two.** `register u8 *tag asm("$10")`,
`register s32 x asm("$23")`, and splitting the raw `obj->x` into its own local
so the subtraction writes a different register - which is what retail does,
holding `obj->x` in `$t0` and `x - viewportX` in `$s7`. Pins alone reach 55;
the split alone is *worse*, at 281 instructions. That is the
"a pin and a statement position are not independent axes" case from
`func_8005B36C`'s entry, and it is why the unpinned form is what is stored
here.

### Opening pass

Pinning the scratchpad tag pointer to `$t2` is worth six, 68 to **62**. Retail
holds the `0x1F800060` constant there and the candidate puts it in `$t0`; the
three scratchpad pointers are compile-time constants, so they are materialised
independently and each can be placed on its own.

Pinning the texture word to `$a1` costs three, and the two coordinate locals
reject their retail registers outright: `x` to `$t0` costs 200 and `y` to `$t1`
two, so the `$s5`/`$t0` exchange on `x` is not reachable by constraining `x`.

**A note on measuring this one.** `func_80031874` is `0x460` bytes, 280
instructions. Passing the wrong size to `fndiff.py` truncates the comparison and
reports a number that looks like a catastrophic regression - 281 against a
baseline of 68 - purely because the streams no longer align. The size comes from
`functions.csv` and the triage tool reads it from there; a manual invocation has
to as well. `tmp/harness/fndiff.py` now refuses a size that disagrees with the
inventory and looks the size up when the argument is omitted, so the mistake
cannot be made silently again.

**Ruled out for the store ordering.** Retail loads `gGraphics_sViewportX` before
the `0x290` and `0xFA` stores and does the subtraction before the two texture
stores; the candidate loads it after. Moving the subtraction ahead of the
`0x290` store, and ahead of the `0x100010` store, are both neutral - GCC
schedules the load where it wants regardless of statement position.

### 68 to 43

- *Pin the scratchpad tag pointer to `$t2`*: 68 to **62**. The three scratchpad
  pointers are compile-time constants, materialised independently, so each can
  be placed on its own.
- *Split the raw screen X from the viewport-adjusted one*: 62 to **59**. Retail
  loads the raw value into `$t0`, a caller-saved register, and computes
  `x - gGraphics_sViewportX` into `$s7`; one variable cannot be in both, and the
  candidate's single `x` was therefore forced into a callee-saved register for
  its whole life. **Splitting `y` the same way costs an instruction** and is
  wrong - retail does reuse one register there - so the two coordinates are not
  symmetric and each has to be read separately.
- *Pin the texture word to `$a1`*: 59 to **57**.

**The rejected pins were misread and the correction matters.** Pinning `x` to
`$t0` appeared to cost 200. It does not: the build is then 276 instructions
against 280, because constraining `x` lets GCC prove two callee-saved registers
unnecessary and drop their save/restore pairs. With a length mismatch the count
measures alignment. Retail uses **all eight** of `$s0`-`$s7`, and the unpinned
candidate matches that pressure exactly, so the pin was over-constraining rather
than being refused.

- *Pin the viewport-adjusted X to `$s7`*: 57 to **44**. This is the other half
  of the split above - once the raw value has its own variable, the adjusted one
  can be placed, and retail keeps it in `$s7` across the whole body. Pinning the
  record index to `$s6` at the same time **adds an instruction** and is wrong,
  so again the two are not symmetric.

With that, the remaining positions are almost entirely ordering in the setup
block: retail issues the `0x290` and `0xFA` sprite stores before loading
`gGraphics_sViewportX`, and the candidate loads the viewport value first. The
`$s7` pin also makes GCC load the viewport value *into* `$s7` and subtract in
place, where retail loads it into `$v1` and writes the difference to `$s7` -
the standing cost of pinning a destination. Naming the viewport value in its own
local is neutral and pinning that local to `$v1` costs six, so the temporary
cannot be forced from the source.

**A near-miss worth recording, because it is the alignment trap wearing a new
costume.** In the window after the setup block the two columns show different
shift/add decompositions of the record stride - the candidate reaching `9x` then
`144x`, the target `128x` then `129x` - which reads exactly like a wrong
multiplier constant in the source. Deriving a corrected stride from the target's
column and substituting it gives 278 instructions against 280 and 256 differing
positions: the constant `0x2D4C` is right.

The columns in that window are **not aligned**, because the differences earlier
in the setup block displace everything after them. Reading two misaligned
streams operation-by-operation manufactures a difference that is not there.
`fndiff.py` prints both streams against the target's addresses, which makes the
rows look comparable when they are not; only the leading matched region can be
read that way. **Fix the earliest difference before interpreting any later
one.**

**The remaining flags are exhausted.** Only `gcc_2_8_1_g8_split_no_strength_reduce`
gives the right instruction count at all - plain `g8_split`, `g8`, `g0_split`
and `g8_split_comm` are all seven to eleven instructions long. Adding
`-fno-schedule-insns`, `-fno-schedule-insns2`, `-fno-cse-follow-jumps` or
`-fno-gcse` to it costs three to five instructions each, so both scheduling
passes and both CSE passes are enabled in retail;
`-fno-expensive-optimizations` is neutral.

**The three opening loads cannot be ordered from the source.** Retail issues
them as screen X, screen Y, texture; the candidate as texture, X, Y. All six
permutations of the three statements were compiled: three give 43 and three give
44, and none gives the target's order. Making the X load `volatile` costs two
instructions, and pinning the raw X to `$t0` was already ruled out. GCC places
these three by its own scheduling and the source has no say in it.

The stored source takes one of the 43 arrangements. Two positions of the
residual are this ordering; the rest follow it, since everything after the first
difference is displaced.

**Ruled out around the opening block.** Inlining the texture read at its two
stores, and reading screen Y directly into its subtraction, each add an
instruction: all three values need to be variables. Pinning the sprite
scratchpad pointer to `$s0` costs eight. Pinning the text scratchpad pointer to
`$s3` is dead - not merely equal in count but **identical in position set**,
which is the check that distinguishes a dead pin from one that has relocated the
problem, and is worth running on any pin whose count does not move.

Reading the three opening values through a typed `struct` view of the object
rather than casts on a `u8 *` is neutral, in count and in position set, so the
spelling of the loads does not reach their order either.

**`volatile` on the *texture* load is inert, which completes that dimension.**
The entry records that making the screen-X load `volatile` costs two
instructions. The untried half was the other side: the source already writes X
before the texture, and GCC hoists the texture load above it, so anchoring the
texture load rather than X is the shape that would help. It does nothing -
`*(volatile u32 *)(obj + 4)` is **byte-identical** to the baseline, same 280
instructions and the same 43 positions.

That matches what `func_80045208`'s entry establishes about this scheduler:
`volatile` orders volatile accesses against each other, but a plain access
moves freely across a volatile one. Here the load that needs to stay put is the
volatile one and the load that needs to rise is plain, which is exactly the
combination `volatile` cannot express. Marking both costs two instructions and
243 positions, the same as marking X alone.

So the opening-load order is now closed against `volatile` from both sides, on
top of the six statement permutations, the struct view, the pin on the raw
value and the inlining already recorded.

### 43 to 29: move the *uses*, not the definitions

Everything above attacks the order of the three opening loads by moving their
**definitions** - six statement permutations, a `volatile` load on either side,
a struct view, a pin on the raw value, inlining each read. All of it is inert,
and the entry concluded that GCC places these three by its own scheduling with
no say from the source.

That conclusion was drawn from the wrong half of the problem. The scheduler
ranks a load by the length of the dependence chain hanging off it, and that
chain is made of the value's **uses**, which nobody had moved. `tex` was
consumed by two stores immediately after the loads while `x0` fed a single
subtraction, so `tex` carried the longer chain and was issued first.

Moving the two texture stores below the `y` subtraction takes the residual from
**43 to 29** with the opcode multiset still identical and no change in
instruction count. Nothing else about the source moved.

Placement of those stores is specific, not merely "later":

| placement of the two `tex` stores | differing |
| --- | --- |
| immediately after the `x` subtraction (previous entry) | 43 |
| **below the `y` subtraction** | **29** |
| below the `rec` computation | 32 |
| immediately after the `idx` read | 45 |
| after `idx`, with the `x` subtraction also moved | 45 |

Moving the `x` subtraction below the `idx` read as well is byte-identical to
the 29 form, so it is the texture stores alone that carry the change.

**The general point is worth taking to other candidates.** When a residual is
the order of two independent loads, permuting the loads is the obvious move and
is usually inert, because their order is not computed from their own position.
It is computed from what depends on them. Six permutations of the definitions
bought nothing here; one placement of a use bought fourteen positions.

### 29 to 26: the same lever, applied to the sprite constants

The gain above came from moving the *uses* of a loaded value. The same move
works on the constants written into the scratchpad sprite. Retail groups the
three word stores together and materialises `0x290` after them; the build
hoists `li $v0,656` ahead of the group, because the halfword store that
consumes it sits close behind.

Moving the two halfword stores at `spr + 0x10` and `spr + 0x12` down past the
`idx` read takes the residual from **29 to 26**, multiset still identical.
Below the `y` subtraction gives the same 26 and an identical position set;
below the `x` subtraction gives 29, so the move has to clear the `idx` read to
pay.

The lever does not generalise to every store in the block, which is worth
recording so it is not applied blindly: moving the two `0xB` halfword stores
the same way costs 55, moving the `tag + 8` word store costs 35, and moving
both costs 54. Only the constants whose materialisation is being hoisted ahead
of the word-store group respond.

### 26 to 16: the record index is read late

Retail reads the row index with `lbu $s6,103($a0)` at `+0x0A8`, *after* the two
texture stores; the candidate read it at `+0x07C`, far ahead of them. Moving
`idx = obj[0x67];` below the texture stores takes the residual from **26 to
16**, multiset still identical.

This is the third value in this function whose *use* position, not its
definition, was the lever, and the three together are worth 27 of the original
43. The pattern is consistent: each time, the candidate computed a value close
to where the source declared it and retail computed it close to where it was
needed, and each time the fix was to move the statement rather than to change
its spelling.

Placement is again specific. Below the two texture stores gives 16; below the
sprite halfword stores gives 29, which is no better than not moving it.

**The viewport split is inert here, on a base where it looked promising.** With
`idx` moved, the remaining cluster is the two `gGraphics_sViewport` loads, which
retail issues earlier than the candidate. Reading either or both into locals
ahead of the sprite halfword stores, and moving the `x` subtraction below them,
are all **byte-identical** to the 16-position build. That is worth recording
because at the 29-position base the same threevariants measured 30, 41 and 30 - so
they were not merely unhelpful there, they were actively harmful, and are now
neutral. The base a lever is measured on changes its cost, which is why a
lever rejected earlier is worth one retry after the surrounding residual moves.

### Two directions closed at the 16-position base

The use-placement lever paid three times, so the next two candidates for it
were tried and both fail, which bounds where it applies.

**The scratchpad text pointer cannot be delayed.** Retail materialises
`lui $s3,0x1f80` at `+0x0E8`; the candidate does it at `+0x098`. `text` is a
constant pointer initialised at its declaration and first used far below, so
moving the assignment down to its use is the obvious fix. Declaring it
uninitialised and assigning it after the `row` computation, before the `rec`
computation, and after it are all **byte-identical** - position set as well as
count. GCC hoists a constant address to the top of the block wherever the
source writes it, which is the same behaviour recorded on `func_800283F4` for
a constant assignment and on `func_80012E5C` for a loop-invariant address.

**The `x` subtraction cannot be moved earlier.** Retail issues
`subu $s7,$t0,$v1` at `+0x09C` where the candidate leaves it to `+0x0E8`, so
hoisting the statement that consumes the viewport value is the natural move.
Placing it above the sprite word stores costs nine instructions and 272
positions; above the first halfword store costs 17. The subtraction is not
free to rise because the viewport load has to rise with it, and that load
competes with the sprite stores for the same slots.

**So the lever is one-directional here.** All three gains came from moving a
use *later*, and both attempts to move one *earlier* are worse or inert. That
is consistent with what the lever does: a value is issued when its consumer
demands it, so delaying the consumer delays the value, while demanding it
sooner only forces the whole dependence chain forward against whatever already
occupies those slots.

**Summary of where this stands.** The residual is 43 of 280, and the earliest
difference - the order of the three opening loads - is proven unreachable: six
statement permutations, a `volatile` load, a struct view, pinning the raw value,
and inlining each read have all been measured. Everything after that point is
*displaced* by it rather than independently wrong, so the true count of
independent errors is smaller than 43 and cannot be read off until the ordering
is solved. The profile and flag space is exhausted; the pin costs are
characterised. Further work needs a lever for GCC's placement of independent
loads, which nothing in this repository's evidence currently provides.
